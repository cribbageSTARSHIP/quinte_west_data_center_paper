## 1. Power Grid Scaling & Ratepayer Protections

* **Grid Interdependency & Hidden Phase 2:** Hydro One’s March 3, 2026 capacity allocation letter to 2874059 Ontario Inc. (All Season Fencing) assigns 18,000 kW out of the Sydney TS-M1 feeder with a strict Assigned Capacity Date of **January 2027**[cite: 1]. However, Page 7 of FirstBlock’s October 2, 2026 Council filing discloses a **33 MW total draw** (16 MW in Phase 1 and 17 MW in Phase 2)[cite: 1].
* **On-Site Generation Asset:** The adjacent co-located parcel at 5 Bernard Long Road holds an active Ontario Energy Board generation licence (**EG-2010-0168**, valid until June 17, 2030) authorizing a **7.5 MW** generation facility[cite: 1].
* **Ratepayer Cost Exposure:** Substation expansions ($35M–$60M) and 230 kV line upgrades ($2.5M–$4.5M/km) risk being transferred to Elexicon and Hydro One Class B residential rate bases under IESO cost-allocation structures if dedicated Connection and Cost Recovery Agreements are not executed[cite: 1, 2].

## 2. Acoustic Vibrations & Low-Frequency Noise (dBC)

* **Tonal Penalty Incurred:** HGC Engineering’s September 9, 2026 Environmental Noise Assessment models 13 outdoor Aermec TBA3350F chillers, 6 diesel generators (107 dBA each), and 6 step-up transformers (83 dBA each)[cite: 1]. Table A1 formally identifies every chiller and transformer as **"T" (Tonal)**, triggering a mandatory +5 dBA penalty that raises each chiller baseline to 94 dBA[cite: 1].
* **The 125 Hz & Infrasound Blindspot:** Page 37 of FirstBlock’s filing specifies an unweighted sound power level of **100.3 dB at 125 Hz**[cite: 1]. Standard dBA weighting filters this down by −16.1 dB to 84.2 dBA, ignoring the wall-vibrating low-frequency pressure wave[cite: 1]. The filing completely omits the 31.5 Hz and 63 Hz octave bands[cite: 1].
* **Ecological & Residential Impact:** The resulting 65–72 dBC valley reverberation disrupts resident sleep architecture and directly interferes with the **100–500 Hz** substrate vibrational frequencies used by honeybees (*Apis mellifera*) for waggle-dance communication across Murray and Sidney Ward orchards[cite: 2, 4].
* **Wiki & Archive Evidence:** `[[raw/articles/04_acoustics_dba_dbc.md]]` | `[[raw/articles/datacenterally-com-blog-why-dba-is-not-the-whole-sound-story.md]]` | `[[raw/articles/loudoun-gov-6405-noise-air-quality-concerns.md]]`[cite: 1, 4]

> ### Direct Question for Proponents:
> *"Why does HGC Engineering's study report compliance exclusively in dBA when your chiller manufacturer data on Page 37 reveals 100.3 dB of unweighted energy at 125 Hz, and will FirstBlock commit to a binding site-plan condition capping boundary noise at 55 dBC with continuous fenceline acoustic monitoring?"*[cite: 1, 2]

## 4. Drinking Water Intakes & Chemical Spill Risks

* **Direct Downstream Threat:** The facility sits immediately upstream of the **Trenton Water Treatment Plant (WTP) intake** (above Dam No. 1, serving ~7,000 ML/year across 8,800 connections) and the **Bayside WTP intake**, placing the site directly inside **Intake Protection Zones IPZ-2** (2-hour travel time) and **IPZ-3** under the Ontario *Clean Water Act, 2006*[cite: 1, 2].
* **Propylene Glycol Biological Oxygen Demand:** Closed-loop cooling chillers hold tens of thousands of liters of propylene glycol, which exerts a Biochemical Oxygen Demand of **1.68 kg O₂ per liter**[cite: 1, 2]. An uncontrolled rupture into the river rapidly strips dissolved oxygen below 2.0 mg/L, suffocating aquatic life and entering the **Bay of Quinte Area of Concern (AOC)**[cite: 1, 2].
* **Bulk Diesel Fuel Hazards:** Six 107 dBA standby diesel generators require tens of thousands of liters of bulk on-site hydrocarbon storage[cite: 1, 2]. Precedents from data center valve failures (e.g., Secaucus, NJ, Sept 2026; 5,000+ gallon spill) demonstrate high contamination risks to connected municipal water supplies[cite: 1].
* **Wiki & Archive Evidence:** `[[concepts/trent-river-drinking-water-intakes.md]]` | `[[raw/articles/trent_river_intakes_and_ipz_source.md]]`[cite: 1, 4]

> ### Direct Question for Proponents:
> *"What is the exact combined volume of propylene glycol and diesel fuel to be stored on the banks of the Trent River, and will FirstBlock engineer 110% capacity impermeable secondary containment vaults with automated pneumatic shut-off valves tied to the Trenton WTP intake alarm system?"*[cite: 1, 2]

## 6. Municipal Tax Deficit & Employment Reality

* **The Machinery Tax Exemption (MPAC):** Under **Section 3(1), paragraph 17 of the Ontario *Assessment Act* (R.S.O. 1990, c. A.31)**, all machinery and equipment used for processing (servers, GPU clusters, high-voltage transformers, switchgear, and UPS systems) is 100% exempt from municipal property taxes[cite: 2, 4].
* **The 'Shell vs. Contents' Deficit:** Over **80% to 90% of the capital expenditure** in a data center is concentrated in tax-exempt compute and power equipment[cite: 2]. Quinte West only collects industrial property taxes on the depreciated physical mill shell and raw land[cite: 2].
* **Automation vs. Job Promises:** Modern data centers operate virtually unstaffed, yielding an industry average of **0.5 to 2.0 permanent jobs per MW** (10 to 25 permanent employees for the entire 18–33 MW facility), refuting claims of broad local industrial employment[cite: 1, 2, 4].
* **Wiki & Archive Evidence:** `[[raw/articles/municipal-tax-deficit-ontario-assessment.md]]`[cite: 1, 4]  
* **Verification Status:** Verified under Ontario Assessment Act R.S.O. 1990 c. A.31 s. 3(1) para 17 machinery exemption and 0.5-2.0 permanent jobs/MW industry baseline[cite: 2, 4].

> ### Direct Question for Proponents:
> *"Under the Assessment Act machinery exemption, what is the exact net property tax revenue Quinte West will collect annually above what the mill shell already pays, and how many permanent full-time jobs are contractually guaranteed to work directly inside the data center if the vertical farm fails to launch?"*[cite: 2]
