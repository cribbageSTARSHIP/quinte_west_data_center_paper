# United States Data Center Contamination & Environmental Enforcement

## 1. Equinix NY2 Bulk Diesel Spill — Secaucus, New Jersey (September 2026)
- **Facility:** Equinix Data Center (NY2 / Hartz Way, Secaucus, Hudson County, New Jersey).
- **Incident Date:** September 11, 2026.
- **Volume Discharged:** ~5,000 to 5,500 gallons of No. 2 diesel fuel.
- **Mechanism of Failure:** Automated system glitch ("computer calling for oil") combined with two failed valves in the fuel filtering/transfer system, allowing diesel to bypass secondary containment boundaries ^[Source: Gothamist | https://gothamist.com/news/broken-valves-caused-a-data-center-to-spill-5k-gallons-of-diesel-in-njs-meadowlands].
- **Receiving Waters:** Anderson Creek (a tidal tributary in the New Jersey Meadowlands that flows directly into the Hackensack River and Newark Bay) ^[Source: Patch Secaucus | https://patch.com/new-jersey/secaucus/data-center-spills-5-500-gallons-diesel-secaucus-creek].
- **Remediation & Oversight:** Clean Harbors emergency response, Secaucus Fire Department, Hudson Regional Health Commission, and the New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection (NJ DEP) ^[Source: The Digest | https://thedigestonline.com/news/secaucus-data-center-diesel-spill-nj/].
- **Municipal & Regulatory Impact:** Secaucus Mayor Michael Gonnelli and Hackensack Riverkeeper initiated Natural Resource Damage (NRD) compensation actions and called on state legislators to establish a moratorium on data center construction in fragile tidal basins ^[Source: NJToday | https://njtoday.news/2026/09/16/5000-gallons-of-diesel-spill-from-secaucus-data-center-into-hackensack-river-tributary/].

## 2. Northern Virginia Data Center Alley Generator Risks (Loudoun & Prince William Counties)
- **Facility Concentration:** Thousands of permitted backup diesel engines across the Dulles corridor aggregate tens of millions of gallons of on-site fuel storage within the Potomac River watershed ^[Source: The Washington Post | https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/05/28/data-centers-boom-virginians-breathe-exhaust-10000-diesel-generators/].
- **Regulatory Action (September 2026):** Virginia DEQ issued a $2.4M Supplemental Environmental Project (SEP) enforcement order to deploy mobile monitoring units and 45 community air sensors following persistent grid stress testing and unpermitted generator run-time exceedances ^[Source: Virginia Department of Environmental Quality / Piedmont Environmental Council].

## 3. Industrial Cooling Tower & Chiller Chemical Releases
- **Process Cooling Discharges:** Facilities using open-loop evaporative cooling towers discharge blowdown water concentrated with biocide agents, scaling inhibitors, and total dissolved solids (TDS) reaching hundreds of thousands of pounds per year into local creeks without pre-treatment.
- **Secondary Glycol Risk:** Closed-loop systems store between 15,000 and 100,000+ gallons of industrial propylene glycol heat-transfer fluids. Accidental releases entering municipal stormwater systems exert extreme Biochemical Oxygen Demand (1.68 kg O2/L), inducing rapid aquatic hypoxia in receiving streams.
