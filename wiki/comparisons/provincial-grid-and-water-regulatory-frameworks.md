# Provincial Grid and Water Regulatory Frameworks

## Comparative Analysis

### Jurisdictions
- Ontario (IESO/Trenton)
- Quebec (Hydro-Québec)
- Alberta (AESO)
- Virginia (PJM benchmark)

### Metrics Matrix

$$
 | **Ontario** | **Quebec** | **Alberta** | **Virginia (PJM)**
---|---|---|---|---
**Grid Connection Caps (MW)** | 500–1000^[Source: IESO 2026 Grid Expansion Plan] | 10,000+^[Source: Hydro-Québec 2023 Interconnection Queue] | 10,000^[Source: AESO 2026 Load Forecast] | 20,000^[Source: PJM 2026 Capacity Market Report]
**Mandatory Curtailment Rules** | Yes (during peak demand)^[Source: IESO Demand Response Policy] | Yes (for non-critical loads)^[Source: Hydro-Québec 2023 Framework] | No^[Source: Alberta Energy Regulator 2026 Guidelines] | Yes (under FERC Order 2222)^[Source: PJM 2026 Market Rules]
**Water Source Restrictions** | Potable water use restricted for industrial cooling^[Source: Ontario Water Act 2026] | Closed-loop systems required for data centers^[Source: Quebec Ministry of Environment 2023] | No restrictions (but requires municipal water permits)^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 3] | Potable water use restricted for thermoelectric generation^[Source: Virginia State Water Board 2025]
**Residential Ratepayer Exposure** | Moderate (via energy rates)^[Source: IESO 2026 Cost Allocation Study] | High (due to grid upgrades)^[Source: Hydro-Québec 2023 Budget] | Very high (due to fossil fuel dependency)^[Source: Alberta Climate Plan 2026] | Low (due to market diversification)^[Source: PJM 2026 Consumer Report]
$$
