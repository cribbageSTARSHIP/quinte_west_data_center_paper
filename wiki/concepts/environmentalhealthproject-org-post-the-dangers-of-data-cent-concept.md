# Environmental Impacts of AI Data Centers

Data centers, particularly those powered by fossil fuels, impose significant environmental and health risks on nearby communities. Key issues include:

1. **Noise Pollution**: Data centers generate noise levels exceeding 90 decibels from construction and operational equipment, leading to hearing loss, stress, and sleep disturbances [[wikilinks]](entities/environmentalhealthproject-org-post-the-dangers-of-data-cent-entity.md). Noise above 85 decibels is harmful to hearing.

2. **Light Pollution**: 24/7 lighting disrupts circadian rhythms, affecting melatonin production and sleep-wake cycles. It also harms wildlife migration patterns [[wikilinks]](concepts/05-human-health.md). Light pollution contributes to increased health risks for humans and ecosystems.

3. **Air Pollution**: Gas-powered data centers emit nitrogen oxides, methane, and particulate matter, linked to respiratory diseases, cardiovascular conditions, and cancer risks. A 2025 study estimates U.S. data centers could cause 600,000 asthma cases and 1,300 premature deaths by 2030 [[wikilinks]](entities/environmentalhealthproject-org-post-the-dangers-of-data-cent-entity.md).

4. **Water Waste**: Large data centers consume up to 5 million gallons of water daily for cooling, exacerbating water insecurity in arid regions. Google's data centers in water-scarce areas highlight this issue [[Source: Title | https://www.environmentalhealthproject.org/post/the-dangers-of-data-centers]].

5. **Economic and Social Equity**: Data centers disproportionately affect minority and low-income communities through pollution exposure and job displacement risks [[Source: Title | https://www.environmentalhealthproject.org/post/the-dangers-of-data-centers]].

This concept page synthesizes findings from the Environmental Health Project's analysis of data center impacts.
