# Environmental Impact Assessments for Data Centers

Environmental impact assessments (EIAs) are critical for evaluating the socioeconomic and ecological impacts of hyperscale AI data centers. In Alberta, EIAs are mandatory for data center projects, though enforcement has been inconsistent. The Alberta Energy Regulator (AER) requires EIAs to address water consumption, thermal discharge, and land use, yet many projects proceed without full assessments [[alberta-water-regulation]]^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 9].

Public concerns include water usage for cooling (estimated at MGD/litres) and potential aquifer depletion. EIAs also assess health impacts from particulate plumes and noise pollution (dBA/dBC levels), though these are often inadequately analyzed [[02-water-ecology]]^[Source: Alberta AI Data Centres Environmental and Regulatory Concerns.pdf, Page 3].

Canadians generally support AI development but demand transparency. 81% are concerned about electricity costs, while 79% worry about environmental impacts, including water consumption and emissions [[mihta-askiy-data-center]]^[Source: AI Data Centers Broad Optimism Meets Local Hesitation.pdf, Page 3].
