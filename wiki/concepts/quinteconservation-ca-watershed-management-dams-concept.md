# Quinte Conservation Watershed Management and Dams

Quinte Conservation manages 40 dams across the Quinte watershed, providing benefits such as flood management, recreation, water supply, and habitat creation. These dams were built decades ago and require ongoing repairs to remain functional. Operating plans dictate when and how dams are managed based on seasonal water levels and ecological needs.

^[Source: Title | https://www.quinteconservation.ca/watershed-management/dams/]

## Dam Operations

- **Spring/Fall Operations**: Dams are operated to lower water levels in the fall to reduce winter ice damage and raise water levels in the spring to maintain summer levels and address droughts.
- **Control Mechanisms**: Dams use stop logs, valves, or inflatable weirs. Some dams, called weirs, cannot be operated and are referred to as “run of the river” dams.
- **Impact on Water Levels**: Factors like rainfall, snowmelt, ice jams, and beaver activity affect water levels. Dams can mitigate flooding by collecting excess water in headponds but are limited by headpond capacity and the location of extreme weather events.

## Hydro-Electric Power

Quinte Conservation operates the McLeod Dam, a hydro-electric facility that provides green energy to power 400 homes. The dam was updated in 2008 with turbines and generators.

^[Source: Title | https://www.quinteconservation.ca/watershed-management/dams/]

## Dam Safety and Maintenance

A Water Control Structure Condition Assessment was conducted to prioritize capital projects over the next decade. Dam Safety Reviews (DSR) are completed under Ministry guidelines to ensure safe operation and public safety.
