Diesel pollution from data centers primarily involves fine particles (PM2.5) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) emissions from backup generators. These pollutants pose significant health risks, including respiratory issues and cardiovascular stress, especially for vulnerable populations. Washington State Department of Ecology regulates data centers through air quality permits to mitigate these risks [[entities/ecology-wa-gov-air-climate-air-quality-data-centers-entity.md]].

Key findings from the 2020 Quincy assessment include:
- Diesel particles from data centers contribute to ambient PM2.5 levels, with highest exposure near generator locations
- NO2 concentrations from data center operations exceed WHO guidelines in proximity to facilities
- Health risk assessments are required before permitting new data center constructions
