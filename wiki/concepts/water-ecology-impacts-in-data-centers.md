Data centers consume vast quantities of water for cooling systems, with hyperscale facilities using 4.2-6.6 billion cubic meters annually by 2027. This strains local water supplies and degrades quality through saline wastewater discharge from evaporative cooling towers, contaminating groundwater with sodium, magnesium, sulfate, and potassium. High sulfate levels correlate with skin irritation and diarrhea in infants, while prolonged drought conditions exacerbate water scarcity, increasing risks of waterborne diseases like E. coli and diarrheal illnesses. In Virginia, data centers consumed 2 billion gallons (7.57 billion liters) in 2023—a 63% increase since 2019—compounding existing water stress from climate change.^[Source: Health implications of the rapid rise of data centers in Virginia an exploratory assessment.pdf, Page 4]
