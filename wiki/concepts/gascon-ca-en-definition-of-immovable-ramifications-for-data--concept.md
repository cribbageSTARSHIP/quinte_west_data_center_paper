### Key Legal Interpretation
Under Quebec's *Act respecting municipal taxation* (ARMT), data centre equipment may be classified as "immovable" if it is **permanently attached** to a building through intellectual connection, even without physical attachment. This was established in two landmark cases:

1. **Ville de Montréal v. Société en commandite Locoshop Angus**
   - Court ruled that data centre infrastructure (cooling systems, power distribution) creates an intellectual link to the building, making it taxable under municipal property assessments.
   - [[entities/gascon-ca-en-definition-of-immovable-ramifications-for-data--entity.md]]

2. **Ville de Québec v. Vidéotron ltée**
   - Wireless telephony equipment was deemed taxable when integrated into building infrastructure.
   - [[entities/quebec-employers-council-entity.md]]

### Tax Implications
- Municipalities can levy property taxes on data centre equipment alongside traditional real estate.
- Equipment valued at high technological costs (e.g., specialized cooling systems) may significantly impact lease negotiations.
- Industry groups like the **Quebec Employers Council** and **Quebec's Federation of Chambers of Commerce** are lobbying for manufacturing-sector tax exemptions.
