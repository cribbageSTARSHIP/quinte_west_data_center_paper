# Evaporative Cooling Loss Mechanisms

Evaporative cooling systems in AI data centers consume significant volumes of municipal water, with losses occurring through three primary pathways: [[concepts/02-water-ecology.md]](thermal discharge)^[Source: Title | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598037/], [[concepts/04-acoustics-dba-dbc.md]](low-frequency resonance)^[Source: Title | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598037/], and [[entities/ncbi-nlm-nih-gov-books-nbk598037-entity.md]](aquifer depletion)^[Source: Title | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598037/].

Quantitative studies indicate that evaporative cooling can account for 30-50% of total water use in hyperscale facilities, with [[concepts/02-water-ecology.md]](municipal water drawdown)^[Source: Title | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598037/] representing the largest single loss category. Thermal discharge from cooling towers, as documented in [[concepts/02-water-ecology.md]](aquifer depletion)^[Source: Title | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598037/], can elevate local water temperatures by 5-15°C, impacting aquatic ecosystems.
