# Emergency Diesel Generator Emissions in Data Centers

Emergency diesel generators in data centers emit 200-600x more nitrogen oxides (NOx) than natural gas plants, with limited use still associated with $300M annual public health costs and 14,000 asthma-related impacts [[1]](https://www.epa.gov/stationary-source). EPA guidelines default to 500 hours of potential emissions for emergency generators under worst-case scenarios [[2]](https://www.epa.gov/stationary-source). These generators release fine PM2.5 and NOx linked to respiratory disease, heart disease, and asthma [[3]](https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-center).

## Regulatory Context
- EPA considers emergency operations exempt from standard emission limits during crises [[4]](https://www.epa.gov/stationary-source).
- 500 hours is the default PTE assumption for emergency generators [[5]](https://www.epa.gov/stationary-source).

## Health Impacts
- Associated with 100,000-200,000 premature deaths/year from PM2.5 exposure [[6]](https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-center).
- Diesel generators used during outages emit 200-600x more NOx than natural gas plants [[7]](https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-center).
