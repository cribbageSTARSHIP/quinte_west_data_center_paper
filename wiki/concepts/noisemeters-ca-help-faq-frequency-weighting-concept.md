Frequency weighting in sound level meters is designed to approximate human auditory perception. The human ear is most sensitive to frequencies between 500 Hz and 8 kHz, with reduced sensitivity to very low (<100 Hz) and high (>8 kHz) frequencies^[Source: noisemeters-ca-help-faq-frequency-weighting.md | https://www.noisemeters.ca/help/faq/frequency-weighting/].

### A-Weighting
- Used for most noise measurement applications
- Cuts off lower and higher frequencies not perceptible to humans
- Expressed as dBA or dB(A)
- Defined in IEC 60651, IEC 60804, IEC 61672, ANSI S1.4
- At higher sound levels (100 dB+), the ear's response becomes flatter (C-weighting)

### C-Weighting
- Used for peak measurements and entertainment noise
- Less attenuation of low frequencies
- Expressed as dBC or dB(C)

### Z-Weighting
- Flat frequency response from 10 Hz to 20 kHz ±1.5dB
- Replaces older "Linear"/"Unweighted" responses
- Expressed as dBZ or dB(Z)
