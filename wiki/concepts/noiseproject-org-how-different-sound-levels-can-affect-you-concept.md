# Noise Project - How Different Sound Levels Can Affect You

## Sound Level Ranges and Health Impacts

### 0 dB to 30 dB
- Equivalent to a ticking watch or normal breathing
- No negative health effects associated
- Correlated with relaxation and good sleep

### 30 dB to 40 dB
- Equivalent to soft whispering or rustling leaves
- No specific health problems but may affect sleep patterns

### 40 dB to 50 dB
- Equivalent to a humming fridge or running stream
- Associated with lack of sleep, headaches, irritability
- Chronic exposure may impair cognitive function in children

### 50 dB to 60 dB
- Equivalent to rain muffling or a conversation
- Increased risk of cardiovascular disease
- 8% increased risk of heart disease per 10 dB

### 60 dB to 70 dB
- Equivalent to laughter or a vacuum
- No immediate hearing loss risk with short exposure
- Prolonged exposure may cause indirect health effects

### 70 dB to 80 dB
- Equivalent to city traffic or a lawn mower
- Associated with Noise Induced Hearing Loss (NIHL)
- Risk increases at 70 dB and above

### 80 dB to 90 dB
- Equivalent to a car horn or farm tractor
- Associated with tinnitus and increased risk for pregnant women
- Recommended maximum exposure: 8 hours

### 90 dB to 100 dB
- Equivalent to a subway train or jackhammer
- Associated with behavioral changes and muscle spasms
- Risk of ruptured eardrums and hearing loss

### 100-150 dB
- Equivalent to a plane or fireworks
- Extremely dangerous with risk of ruptured eardrums
- 70% of miners experience hearing impairment by age 60

## Prevention
- Use noise refuges (parks, libraries)
- Use personal protective equipment (ear plugs)
- Advocate for noise pollution policy
- Use the Noise Project app to map sound levels
