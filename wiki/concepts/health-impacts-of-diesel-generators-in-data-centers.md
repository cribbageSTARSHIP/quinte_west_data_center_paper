Diesel generators in data centers emit fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and nitrogen oxides (NOx), which are linked to respiratory disease, asthma, and other health risks. These pollutants can cause breathing difficulties, especially for individuals with pre-existing lung conditions. The Washington State Department of Ecology noted that diesel exhaust contains particles too small to be filtered by the nose and upper respiratory system, leading to lung damage and chemical changes. Additionally, nitrogen dioxide (NO2) can worsen asthma symptoms and contribute to acid rain and smog. Studies estimate that backup generator use could result in nearly $300 million in annual public health costs and 14,000 asthma-related health impacts across multiple states.^[Source: Diesel pollution from data centers.pdf, Page 4]
