# Power Grid Strain in Alberta

Alberta's data centers face unique challenges due to tax incentives that reduce municipal revenue while increasing power demand. The government imposes a 2% levy on computing equipment, but this is fully deductible against corporate taxes, effectively subsidizing hyperscale operations. This creates a paradox where facilities contribute little to provincial revenues while straining the grid. [[power-grid-strain]]^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 3].

Key findings:
- Alberta's power grid faces surging demand from data centers, with load requests reaching 20,000 MW—exceeding the province's 10,000 MW generation capacity [[grid-strain-from-cumulative-load]]^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 3].
- Tax abatements and PILOT agreements allow data centers to defer municipal taxes for up to 15 years, reducing local revenue to fund infrastructure [[municipal-tax-revenue]]^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 3].
