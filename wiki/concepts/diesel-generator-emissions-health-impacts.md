Diesel generators used as backup power in data centers emit nitrogen oxides (NOx) and fine particulate matter (PM2.5), which contribute to smog formation and are linked to increased rates of respiratory and cardiovascular diseases^[Source: Analyzing air pollution health economic risks from AI data centers Harvard TH Chan School of Public.pdf, Page 6]^. According to the University of California, Riverside study, health impacts from pollution associated with data centers tripled from 2019 to 2023^[Source: California data center health impacts tripled in 4 years.pdf, Page 1]^. The report recommends phasing out diesel generators in favor of battery storage paired with renewables to reduce emissions and health risks^[Source: California data center health impacts tripled in 4 years.pdf, Page 2]^.
