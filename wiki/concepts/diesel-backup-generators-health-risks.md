### Health Risks from Diesel Backup Generators in Data Centers

Diesel backup generators, commonly used in data centers to maintain operations during power outages, pose significant health risks due to their emissions. These generators emit nitrogen dioxide and particulate matter, which are linked to respiratory issues and premature deaths. A 2026 report by CEDS found that communities near data centers face increased exposure to these pollutants, with risks amplified in areas hosting multiple facilities.^[Source: Data centers air pollution associated with lung issues Report.pdf, Page 2]

### Regulatory and Community Concerns

The Pollution Control Hearings Board (PCHB) mandated assessments of diesel particle pollution in Quincy, Oregon, to evaluate community health risks. Such efforts highlight the need for public health assessments before new data center developments, as recommended by CEDS. Additionally, the U.S. EPA and DOE have emphasized the need for responsible energy use and emission controls to mitigate health impacts.^[Source: Diesel pollution from data centers.pdf, Page 2]
