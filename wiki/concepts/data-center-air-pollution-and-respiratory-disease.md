Air pollution from data centers, particularly from diesel generators, has significant impacts on respiratory health. Diesel exhaust contains PM2.5 and NOx, which are associated with respiratory disease, heart disease, and asthma. Exposure to these pollutants can lead to breathing problems, even in short durations. In Virginia, an analysis found that limited backup generator use was linked to nearly $300 million in annual public health costs and 14,000 asthma-related health impacts. Nitrogen dioxide (NO2) can make breathing harder for people with asthma and contributes to acid rain and smog. These health risks highlight the need for cleaner power alternatives and stricter emissions regulations in data center operations.^[Source: From Energy Use to Air Quality the Many Ways Data Centers Affect US Communities.pdf, Page 6]
