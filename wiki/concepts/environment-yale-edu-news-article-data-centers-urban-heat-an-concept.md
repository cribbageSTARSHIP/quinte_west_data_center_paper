Data centers contribute significantly to urban heat through waste heat emissions, with large facilities potentially increasing surrounding land surface temperatures by 16°F and air temperatures by 4°F. This exacerbates the urban heat island effect, raising energy demand for air conditioning and health risks in vulnerable communities. [[wikilinks]](entities/environment-yale-edu-news-article-data-centers-urban-heat-an-entity.md) ^[Source: Title | https://environment.yale.edu/news/article/data-centers-urban-heat-and-ai-growth]

Yale School of the Environment research highlights solutions such as improved facility design, neighborhood cooling strategies (parks, reflective surfaces), and life-cycle sustainability metrics for data centers. These efforts aim to balance AI growth with environmental stewardship.
