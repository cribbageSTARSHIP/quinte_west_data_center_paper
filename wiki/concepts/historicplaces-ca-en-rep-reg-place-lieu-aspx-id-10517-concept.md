The Lockmaster’s House, located at Sidney Lock Station on the Trent-Severn Waterway, exemplifies early 20th-century residential architecture influenced by the Arts and Crafts Movement. Its design integrates functional requirements for both a lockmaster’s residence and operational space, featuring a one-and-a-half storey bungalow with a steeply pitched roof, dormer windows, and a full-width verandah. The building’s construction materials—brick, clapboard, and shingles—reflect durable, vernacular craftsmanship typical of the era. 

This structure maintains an unchanged relationship to its site, aligning with the environmental and historical value of the Trent-Severn Waterway National Historic Site. Its aesthetic and functional design contribute to the park-like setting of the Lock Station, preserving a visual and spatial connection to the canal and dam infrastructure.
