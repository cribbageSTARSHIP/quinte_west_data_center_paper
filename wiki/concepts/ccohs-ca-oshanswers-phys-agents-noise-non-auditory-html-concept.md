# Non-Auditory Effects of Noise

## Physiological Effects
- **Startle response**: Muscular activity triggered by loud noise for protective purposes
- **Muscle tension**: Contraction of muscles in presence of loud noise
- **Respiratory reflexes**: Changes in breathing patterns when noise is present
- **Cardiovascular changes**: Altered heart rate patterns and blood vessel diameter (particularly in skin)
- These responses mirror physiological reactions to other stressors

## Performance Impacts
### Speech Intelligibility
- Background noise above 50 dB(A) impairs speech understanding
- Communication capabilities degrade as noise levels increase:
  | Communication Type | Max Background Noise |
  |---|---|
  | Face-to-face | <50 dB(A) for normal voice at 6m |
  | Telephone | <70 dB(A) for satisfactory communication |
  | Intercom | <70 dB(A) for satisfactory use |
  | Public Address | <90 dB(A) for satisfactory use |

### Annoyance Factors
- **Acoustic factors**: Sound level, frequency, duration
- **Secondary factors**: Spectral complexity, sound fluctuations, noise localization
- **Nonacoustic factors**: Individual differences, noise necessity, predictability
- WHO guidelines: 50-55 dBA for daytime to prevent moderate/serious annoyance

### Job Interference
- Distraction from conversations affecting concentration
- Risk of missed audible alarms
- Sleep disruption from occupational noise impacting workplace alertness
