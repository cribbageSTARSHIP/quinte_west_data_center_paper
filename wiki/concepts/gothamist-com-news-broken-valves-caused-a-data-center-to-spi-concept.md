# Data Center Fuel System Spill Containment

This page synthesizes knowledge about diesel fuel system failures in data centers, focusing on containment strategies and regulatory implications. The incident described in the source involved 5,000 gallons of diesel spilled due to broken valves in New Jersey's Meadowlands.

## Key Containment Mechanisms

- **Secondary Containment Ponds**: Required by EPA regulations for facilities storing >1,000 gallons of diesel. The Meadowlands incident highlighted gaps in secondary containment infrastructure.
- **Spill Detection Sensors**: Modern facilities use IoT-enabled sensors to detect leaks within 30 seconds of occurrence.
- **Regulatory Framework**: New Jersey's Spill Prevention Control and Countermeasure (SPCC) rules require facilities to have spill response plans and secondary containment for aboveground storage tanks.

## Incident Analysis

^[Source: Title | https://gothamist.com/news/broken-valves-caused-a-data-center-to-spill-5k-gallons-of-diesel-in-njs-meadowlands] The Meadowlands spill demonstrated the need for:

1. Regular valve integrity testing
2. Automated shutdown systems
3. Employee training on emergency protocols
