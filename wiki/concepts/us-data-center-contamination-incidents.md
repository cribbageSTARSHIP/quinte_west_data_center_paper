# United States Data Center Contamination Case Studies

## 1. Equinix NY2 Bulk Diesel Spill (Secaucus, New Jersey — September 11, 2026)
- **Facility / Operator:** Equinix NY2 Data Center campus, Hartz Way, Secaucus, Hudson County, New Jersey ^[Source: Patch Secaucus | https://patch.com/new-jersey/secaucus/data-center-spills-5-500-gallons-diesel-secaucus-creek].
- **Incident Summary:** Approximately 5,000 to 5,500 gallons of No. 2 diesel fuel escaped an on-site generator storage tank and distribution system during facility expansion ^[Source: PCMag | https://www.pcmag.com/news/data-center-spills-thousands-of-gallons-of-diesel-into-nj-creek-after-computer].
- **Mechanism of Failure:** An automated fuel management system malfunction ("computer calling for oil") combined with two broken/failed valves in the fuel filtration loop, allowing fuel to overflow containment boundaries ^[Source: Gothamist | https://gothamist.com/news/broken-valves-caused-a-data-center-to-spill-5k-gallons-of-diesel-in-njs-meadowlands].
- **Receiving Receptor:** Anderson Creek (Sack Creek), a 52-acre tidal wetland marsh and tributary of the Hackensack River draining through the New Jersey Meadowlands toward Newark Bay.
- **Emergency Containment:** Secaucus Fire Department, Hudson Regional Health Commission, and NJ Department of Environmental Protection (NJ DEP) coordinated with remediation contractor Clean Harbors, deploying containment booms and skimming ~3,500 gallons of fuel/water mixture in initial operations.
- **Legal & Policy Fallout:** Secaucus Mayor Michael Gonnelli and Hackensack Riverkeeper Bill Sheehan initiated Natural Resource Damage (NRD) recovery claims and joined environmental coalitions calling for a statewide moratorium on data center construction in sensitive watersheds ^[Source: CBS News New York | https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/secaucus-nj-data-center-fuel-spill/].

## 2. Northern Virginia Data Center Alley Secondary Containment & Emissions Baselines
- **Aggregate Diesel Volumes:** With over 10,000 permitted standby diesel engines across Loudoun and Prince William counties, aggregate on-site bulk fuel storage exceeds tens of millions of gallons in the Potomac River watershed.
- **Regulatory Action:** In September 2026, the Virginia Department of Environmental Quality (VA DEQ) executed a $2.4M Supplemental Environmental Project (SEP) enforcement order to deploy dedicated mobile monitoring stations and 45 community air sensors following persistent grid stress testing and unpermitted generator run-time exceedances.
