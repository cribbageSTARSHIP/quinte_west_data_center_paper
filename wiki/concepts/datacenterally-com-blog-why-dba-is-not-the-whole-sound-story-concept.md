# Acoustic Measurement Weighting Standards

The distinction between dBA and dBC measurements is critical for accurate noise assessment in data center environments. While both metrics measure sound pressure levels, they apply different frequency weightings:

- **A-weighting (dBA)**: Emphasizes frequencies audible to the human ear at moderate sound levels (20-20,000 Hz), filtering out very low and very high frequencies. Widely used in environmental regulations and community noise ordinances.
- **C-weighting (dBC)**: Provides a flatter response across a broader frequency range (20 Hz - 12.5 kHz), capturing more low-frequency energy. Essential for identifying tonal or low-frequency components that may be masked by dBA measurements.

## Why Both Metrics Matter

A single dBA value can conceal low-frequency dominance, as demonstrated by two equipment layouts with identical dBA readings but different frequency profiles. For example:

> "Imagine two proposed equipment layouts with the same overall A-weighted level at a home. One has a prominent low-frequency tone and the other does not. The overall number alone cannot describe that difference." ^[Source: Title | https://www.datacenterally.com/blog/why-dba-is-not-the-whole-sound-story]

## Practical Applications

- **Regulatory Compliance**: Use dBA for established framework and dBC for low-frequency analysis when assessing potential impacts on sensitive locations (homes, schools).
- **Measurement Context**: Always tie commitments to defined receptors (property lines, schools) and verification methods (independent verification, post-construction testing).
