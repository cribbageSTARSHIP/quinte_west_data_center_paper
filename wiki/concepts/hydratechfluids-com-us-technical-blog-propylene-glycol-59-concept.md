# Propylene Glycol Antifreeze Properties and Applications

## Technical Properties
Propylene Glycol (PG) is a non-toxic, food-safe glycol with a LD50 >20,000mg/kg BW, making it relatively harmless compared to Ethylene Glycol^[Source: https://www.hydratechfluids.com/us/technical-blog/propylene-glycol/59]. It is produced via a reaction between Propylene Oxide and a catalyst with global capacity exceeding 1.5 million tonnes/year. PG has a lower heat transfer efficiency than Ethylene Glycol but offers enhanced safety profiles due to its non-toxic classification.

## Application Requirements
Hydratech recommends a minimum 25-30% v/v concentration for freeze protection below -10°C, with lower concentrations (e.g., 2-3%) sufficient for milder requirements. This concentration range ensures optimal corrosion protection, pH buffering against degradation, and biological control (inhibiting microbial growth at ≥20% concentrations)^[Source: https://www.hydratechfluids.com/us/technical-blog/propylene-glycol/59].

## Environmental Considerations
PG is readily biodegradable in water/soil (half-life 1-5 days) with >60% degradation in 10 days. It is not volatile (vapor pressure <1 mmHg) but may impact aquatic life at extreme concentrations (>18,000 mg/l LC50 for fish)^[Source: https://www.hydratechfluids.com/us/technical-blog/propylene-glycol/59].

## Safety and Regulation
PG is classified as GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) in the USA and meets USP/EP/JP pharmacopeia standards. However, it is not recognized as a food additive in Europe. The OECD SIDS assessment confirms no acute toxicity, mutagenicity, or carcinogenicity in studies spanning 2 years in rats and mice^[Source: https://www.hydratechfluids.com/us/technical-blog/propylene-glycol/59].
