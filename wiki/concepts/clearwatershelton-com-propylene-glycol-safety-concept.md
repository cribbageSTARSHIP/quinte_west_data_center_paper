# Propylene Glycol Safety Measures and Hazards

## Health and Environmental Risks
Propylene glycol exposure poses serious health risks including [1] central nervous system effects, kidney failure, and respiratory irritation according to WHO and CDC. Ethylene glycol toxicity requires immediate medical intervention, with OSHA setting exposure limits at 25 ppm (TWA) and 50 ppm (STEL). Environmental contamination risks include soil and water pollution, with NJ Health reporting glycol's slow degradation in water systems.

^[Source: https://clearwatershelton.com/propylene-glycol-safety/]

## Safety Protocols
Key handling protocols include:
- Personal protection with PPE (gloves, goggles, ventilators)
- Emergency response procedures for ingestion/spills
- Spill containment with proper disposal protocols
- Fire safety measures with foam/CO₂ extinguishers

^[Source: https://clearwatershelton.com/propylene-glycol-safety/]

## Regulatory Compliance
NFPA 13/25 mandates UL-listed antifreeze in fire systems, with annual testing requirements. Environmental regulations require proper hazardous waste disposal under CT DEEP and MassDEP guidelines.
