# Acoustic Measurement Weighting Standards (A-weighting vs C-weighting)

Environmental noise measurement standards use different frequency weighting scales to model human perception of sound. The two primary scales are:

1. **dBA (A-weighting)**: Simulates human hearing sensitivity across the audible spectrum (20 Hz - 20 kHz). Emphasizes mid-range frequencies (1 kHz - 4 kHz) where human hearing is most sensitive. Used for: Noise pollution monitoring, occupational safety limits, community noise complaints.

2. **dBC (C-weighting)**: Measures low-frequency sounds (20 Hz - 1 kHz) with minimal frequency weighting. Detects bass-heavy noise from machinery, transportation, and industrial processes. Used for: Low-frequency noise regulation, acoustic impact assessments near residential areas.

## Key Differences

| Metric          | dBA (A-weighting)        | dBC (C-weighting)       |
|------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Frequency Range  | 20 Hz - 20 kHz        | 20 Hz - 1 kHz           |
| Human Hearing Model | Simulates mid-range hearing | Simulates low-frequency perception |
| Common Use Cases | Noise pollution limits, OSHA standards | Industrial noise assessment, transportation noise |
| Regulatory Context | 2019 EPA Noise Policy (US), WHO guidelines | 2022 EU Low-Frequency Noise Directive |

## Technical Implications

- **DBA measurements** may underestimate low-frequency noise from data centers, wind farms, or HVAC systems.
- **DBC measurements** are critical for detecting subsonic noise from large-scale infrastructure projects.
- Regulatory agencies increasingly require **both dBA and dBC measurements** for comprehensive noise impact assessments.
