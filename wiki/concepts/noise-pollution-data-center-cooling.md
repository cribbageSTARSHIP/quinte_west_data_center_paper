Data center cooling systems, including air chillers and cooling fans, generate persistent low-frequency humming sounds (40-59 decibels) that affect residential areas [[wikilinks:epa-regulatory-standards-for-data-centers]]. While these levels are below hearing-damaging thresholds, they approach EPA thresholds for reduced work/sleep efficiency [[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger..., Page 5]].

Industrial-scale noise complaints in Virginia include descriptions of a continuous 'drone' or 'hum' from cooling infrastructure [[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger..., Page 4]]. Chiller fan noise and vibrations are particularly problematic in densely populated areas [[wikilinks:diesel-generator-emissions-health-impacts]].

Municipal noise ordinances typically set thresholds between 40-59 decibels for industrial operations [[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger..., Page 5]]. Some residents report sleep disturbances and reduced outdoor usage due to persistent noise from data center operations [[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger..., Page 5]].
