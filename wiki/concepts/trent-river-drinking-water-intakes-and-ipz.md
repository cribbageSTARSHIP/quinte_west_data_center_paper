# Trent River Drinking Water Intakes and Intake Protection Zones (IPZ-1, IPZ-2, IPZ-3)

## Surface Water Intake Protection Zones (IPZ)
The Trent River corridor is divided into three Intake Protection Zones (IPZ-1, IPZ-2, IPZ-3) to safeguard drinking water intakes from contamination. These zones are defined by scientific assessments of contamination risks, with IPZ-1 being the most vulnerable (closest to intakes) and IPZ-3 the least vulnerable (further downstream). Each zone has a vulnerability score based on proximity to intakes, contaminant travel time, and intake depth.

^[Source: raw/articles/trent_river_intakes_and_ipz_source.md, Page 2]

## Significant Drinking Water Threats under the Ontario Clean Water Act
Threats include:
- **Spills**: Chemical spills (e.g., DNAPLs) from industrial activities pose acute contamination risks.
- **Hazardous Waste Storage**: Improper storage of toxic substances (e.g., motor oil, solvents) near intakes increases pollution risk.
- **Chemical Storage**: Industrial sites storing volatile organic compounds (VOCs) within IPZ-1 require strict containment protocols.

^[Source: raw/articles/trent_river_intakes_and_ipz_source.md, Page 5]

## Downstream Pathways to Trenton Drinking Water System
Contaminants in IPZ-1 can travel through the Trent River to the **Trenton Drinking Water System**, which draws intake water from the river. From there, contaminants may reach the **Bay of Quinte** via:
1. **Direct riverine transport** (IPZ-1 → Trenton intakes → Bay of Quinte)
2. **Groundwater seepage** from riverbanks into aquifers feeding the Trenton system.
