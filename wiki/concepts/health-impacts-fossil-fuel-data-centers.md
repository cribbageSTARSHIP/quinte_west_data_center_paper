This page synthesizes findings from Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health analyses of air pollution health risks from AI data centers. Key findings include:

1. Fossil fuel-powered data centers contribute to fine particulate matter (PM2.5) pollution, estimated to drive 90% of health impacts from air pollution (^[Source: Analyzing air pollution health economic risks from AI data centers Harvard TH Chan School of Public.pdf, Page 3])
2. Health damages include 3.4-6.5 additional premature deaths/year in densely populated areas (^[Source: Analyzing air pollution health economic risks from AI data centers Harvard TH Chan School of Public.pdf, Page 5])
3. Vulnerable populations include those with asthma, lower income, and pre-existing conditions (^[Source: Analyzing air pollution health economic risks from AI data centers Harvard TH Chan School of Public.pdf, Page 7])
4. Economic costs include $53-99 million/year in health damages for facilities in high-population areas (^[Source: Analyzing air pollution health economic risks from AI data centers Harvard TH Chan School of Public.pdf, Page 4])
