# Source Content

Best lakes around Quinte West are defined by their connection to the expansive Bay of Quinte and Lake Ontario. This region is a significant part of the Trent-Severn Waterway, offering a network of natural waterways. The area provides diverse aquatic environments, from large bays to smaller inland lakes and rivers. These features contribute to the region's appeal for various outdoor activities.

Presqu'ile Provincial Park is a stunning swath of protected Lake Ontario waterfront spanning 937 hectares. There's a massive 2.5-kilometre sandy beach, freshwater marshes, thriving secondary forests, and it's a major migration hotspot for over 335 species of birds and thousands of Monarch butterflies every fall. With hiking and biking routes, there are also over 300 campsites spread across 8 distinct campground loops. Be aware of the time of the year you visit as all visitors entering the park require a valid vehicle permit, and busy summer weekends and autumn migration periods get quite impacted. During crowded times, reserve your Daily Vehicle Permit (DVP) online up to five days in advance to guarantee entry here https://ontarioparks.ca/dayuse/getapermit

The Owen Point Trail is a short but stunning trail exploring the tip southern of Presqu'ile Provincial Park. It is a famous bird and shorebird-viewing trail in the park, offering front-row views of the expansive Lake Ontario shoreline and Gull Island. The trail is easy, flat and family friendly, made up of a mixture of packed dirt paths, sand, and wood chips passing though a coastal mix of dense dogwood shrubbery, with spur trails to open gravel beaches. The trail also features five numbered viewing stations placed at strategic lookouts along the shore for birdwatching.
Zwick’s Island Park is that largest waterfront park in Belleville, and sits right where the Moira River meets the Bay of Quinte. There are walking trails, picnic areas, playgrounds, open green spaces, and plenty of views across the water, making it a popular spot for recreation and community events. It’s also an excellent place to watch boats and waterbirds along the busy shoreline.
This waterside park stretches along the water with amenities such as a playground, boat launch, and access to the Waterfront Trail. It's a great place for families to visit since it's not far from downtown Belleville.
The region offers diverse activities. The Bay of Quinte and Lake Ontario are excellent for fishing, boating, and paddling. The Trent River, part of the historic Trent-Severn Waterway, provides opportunities for fishing and riverside hiking. Smaller lakes like Oak Lake and Rice Lake are popular for fishing. You can also find beaches and trails at places like Presqu’ile Provincial Park.
Yes, several locations are ideal for families. Presqu’ile Provincial Park offers a large sandy beach, marsh boardwalks, and easy hiking trails. Zwick’s Island Park in Belleville features walking trails, picnic areas, and playgrounds right where the Moira River meets the Bay of Quinte. Kiwanis East Bayshore Park also provides a playground and access to the Waterfront Trail.
You'll find several options for hiking. Presqu’ile Provincial Park has multiple trails, including the easy and scenic Owen Point Trail, known for birdwatching. The Trent River banks also offer trails like the Jack Lange Memorial Walkway. For more options, explore easy hikes in the area via the Easy hikes around Quinte West guide.
The Bay of Quinte is a renowned fishing hotspot, particularly for walleye and bass, hosting major fishing events. The Trent River is also a recommended spot for largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, and walleye. For bass fishing, Oak Lake and Rice Lake are noted in fishing reports. East Lake and West Lake in Prince Edward County are also excellent for various species including bass, walleye, pike, and perch.
Yes, cycling is a popular activity. The Waterfront Trail is accessible from places like Kiwanis East Bayshore Park. Presqu’ile Provincial Park offers biking routes. You can find various cycling routes, including road cycling and touring options, in the Road Cycling Routes around Quinte West and Cycling around Quinte West guides.
The Trent River is a crucial part of the historic Trent-Severn Waterway, a 386-kilometer system of rivers, lakes, and canals. Quinte West serves as the southern gateway, with the first seven locks of this national historic site located within the city. Presqu'ile Provincial Park also features one of the oldest operating lighthouses in the province, adding a historic landmark to the Lake Ontario shoreline.
The cities of Belleville and Quinte West offer picturesque views overlooking the Bay of Quinte. Sager Conservation Area provides panoramic views of the Trent River Valley from its lookout tower. Presqu’ile Provincial Park, particularly the Owen Point Trail, offers expansive views of Lake Ontario and Gull Island.
Yes, Presqu’ile Provincial Park boasts a massive 2.5-kilometer sandy beach on Lake Ontario. Additionally, East Lake and West Lake in neighboring Prince Edward County provide access to the renowned sandy beaches of Sandbanks Provincial Park, which are popular for swimming and sunbathing.
Summer is popular for boating, swimming, and general outdoor activities. Spring and fall are excellent for birdwatching, especially at Presqu'ile Provincial Park, which is a major migration hotspot. Fishing is also popular throughout the warmer months, with specific derbies often held in spring and fall.
Absolutely. The Bay of Quinte is a popular spot for pleasure boating, with various boat launches available, including at Centennial Park and the Albert Street Boat Launch. Kayaking and canoeing are also popular, with rentals often available. The Trent River, as part of the Trent-Severn Waterway, allows for enjoyable paddling or motoring experiences between its locks.
While the Bay of Quinte can be busy, smaller lakes like Oak Lake and Rice Lake are primarily known for fishing and may offer a more tranquil experience compared to the larger, more popular waterways. Exploring the less-trafficked sections of the Trent River or specific conservation areas along its banks, such as Glen Miller Conservation Area, can also provide a quieter natural setting.
