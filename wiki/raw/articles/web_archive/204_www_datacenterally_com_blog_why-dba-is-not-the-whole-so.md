# Source Content

dBA and dBC are frequency weightings, not competing verdicts. A useful data center sound review connects both the metric and the measurement method to the sound people may experience at homes, schools, and other sensitive locations.
Can you convert dBA to dBC?
There is no fixed conversion. You need the sound’s frequency content or measurements using both weightings. Adding a standard number to a dBA reading cannot establish the dBC level. OSHA’s technical explanation of weighting networks describes the distinction; its workplace limits are not neighborhood noise standards.
What dBA emphasizes
The A-weighted scale reduces the contribution of lower and very high frequencies to better approximate human hearing at common sound levels. It is widely used in ordinances and environmental reviews, making it useful for comparison and compliance. But a single overall dBA value can conceal a distinct hum, tone, or low-frequency component.
What dBC adds
The C-weighted scale is flatter across a broader frequency range and gives more weight to low-frequency energy than dBA. A notable gap between dBC and dBA can be a prompt for more analysis, but the gap alone does not diagnose a problem. Octave-band or one-third-octave-band data, tonal analysis, duration, and background conditions provide the needed context.
A simple example: the same dBA, a different sound
Imagine two proposed equipment layouts with the same overall A-weighted level at a home. One has a prominent low-frequency tone and the other does not. The overall number alone cannot describe that difference. Ask the acoustician for frequency-band results, tonal assessment and the operating conditions behind each prediction. This example is illustrative, not a measurement from a listed project.
Model the real operating cases
Sound sources can include rooftop mechanical equipment, cooling systems, transformers, substations, air-handling equipment, and emergency-generator tests. Reviews should identify simultaneous worst-case operation, nighttime conditions, seasonal modes, barriers, terrain, reflections, and meteorological assumptions. Manufacturer data should be traceable to the equipment and model inputs.
Verify at community receptors
Define measurement points before approval: property lines, the nearest residence, schools, outdoor-use areas, and any location where terrain or building reflections matter. Specify weather limits, instrument settings, time averaging, background correction, post-construction testing, complaint intake, corrective deadlines, and who pays for independent verification.
Check the rule for the actual site
A measurement method is not a universal neighborhood limit. Identify the local ordinance, approval conditions, measurement location and effective date. Our dated Virginia policy guide provides a starting point; confirm current requirements in the original local and state records.
Use dBA for the established framework, add dBC and frequency analysis when low-frequency or tonal sound is plausible, and tie every commitment to a defined receptor and verification method.
