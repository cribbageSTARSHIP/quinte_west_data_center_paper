In spring and summer 2026, the Department of Energy (DOE),
acting pursuant to Federal Power Act section 202(c), issued a
series of emergency orders
directing Regional Transmission Organizations (RTOs) and other
energy providers to take certain actions during periods of high
demand due to extremely hot weather conditions to prevent firm
load interruption, which could lead to adverse public health and
safety impacts. In these orders, DOE directed certain entities to
maximize the generation output levels of specified units and/or to
require data centers and other large load customers to operate
their back-up generation (e.g., stationary engines) as a last resort
before declaring an Energy Emergency Alert Level 3.

Power sources are a major concern for planning data centers and
SI infrastructure. Stationary combustion turbines and stationary
engines – common sources of primary and backup power for data
centers – are subject to various new source performance
standards (NSPS) for certain air emissions and national emission
standards for hazardous air pollutants (NESHAP). Portable
engines (known as nonroad engines) are also subject to air
emission standards.
