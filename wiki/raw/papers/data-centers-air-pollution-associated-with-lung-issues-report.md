Data centers’ air pollution associated
with lung issues: Report
Katie Millard
3–4 minutes
COLUMBUS, Ohio (WCMH) — Emissions from data centers can
cause breathing issues and premature deaths for those living
nearby, according to a new report.
The report was compiled by Community & Environmental Defense
Services (CEDS) founder and president Richard Klein, who has
been working in development-related concerns for 40 years. Klein
found that a single data center could pose negative health risks for
people living at least 0.6 miles away, sometimes further.
Klein said risks increase when a home is near multiple data
centers.
A growing number of Americans in the central U.S. live near one
or more data centers or will in the near future. Data centers are
massive buildings that store computing equipment and support IT
servers, data storage and networks.
The centers are necessary to support the digital infrastructure that
modern society relies on. However, some people take issue with
data center construction near their homes and schools, citing
pollution and health concerns.

Klein said air pollution risks come from data centers emitting
nitrogen dioxide and tiny, inhalable particles that can harm
people’s lungs. According to the National Institute of Health, the
particles cause between 100,000 and 200,000 premature deaths
each year.
Data centers require vast amounts of power, and the influx of the
complexes has caused some people’s energy bills to go up. With
rising electric costs drawing public frustration, developers are
beginning to pursue alternative power options, which can bring
additional pollutants in turn.
The centers also cannot lose power without risking vast amounts
of data, so they may rely on emergency backup generators. Many
data centers use diesel generators as backup power, which are
particularly strong polluters.
Klein recommends that states require public health assessments
for neighboring communities before a new complex is built.
Data centers and the controversy surrounding them came up
during Tuesday night’s State of the Union address. President
Trump said he would press tech companies involved in artificial
intelligence to pay higher electricity rates in areas where their data
centers are located. The buildings tend to use large volumes of
electricity, potentially increasing the cost of power to other
consumers in the area.
The White House will host major tech companies involved in
building out the centers for artificial intelligence on March 4 for
them to sign a pledge to protect consumers from higher electricity
prices.
The Associated Press contributed to this report.
