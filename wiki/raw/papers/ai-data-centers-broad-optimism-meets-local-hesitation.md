Chloé Bergeron

It is widely understood that artificial intelligence is advancing
quickly. Less attention is often paid to the physical infrastructure
needed to support that growth, including the data centres that
power AI systems.
As governments and technology companies invest in new AI data
centres across Canada, public opinion suggests Canadians are
open to the idea, but not without reservations.
Our latest Leger survey finds that while many Canadians see AI
data centres as a positive development, concerns about electricity
demand, environmental impacts, and who should bear the costs
remain front of mind.

Key Highlights
Awareness of AI data centres is already well established, with 62%
of Canadians saying they had heard about them before taking the
survey.
Canadians are more likely to view AI data centre development
positively than negatively, with 46% calling it a good thing and 37%
calling it a bad thing.
Support becomes more divided at the provincial level, where 44%
support development and 42% oppose it.
Affordability and sustainability shape public opinion, with 81%
concerned about higher household electricity bills and 79%
concerned about environmental impacts.
Canadians expect AI companies to take responsibility for the
electricity and infrastructure their operations require.

Canadians generally recognize the potential value of investing in
AI infrastructure. Nearly half, 46%, believe the development of AI
data centres in Canada is a good thing. Positive perceptions are
especially strong among Quebecers, men, and Canadians aged
55 and older.
Opinions become more divided when development is proposed
within respondents’ own province. Nationally, 44% support AI data
centres in their province, while 42% oppose them.
This close split suggests that broad support for AI does not
automatically translate into support for local projects. Electricity
demand, infrastructure pressures, environmental effects, and
community benefits are likely to influence how future
developments are received.
Canadians Expect Companies To Help Cover The
Costs
Affordability and sustainability are the most consistent concerns
across the survey.
More than eight in ten Canadians, 81%, are concerned that AI
data centres could increase household electricity bills. A similar
proportion, 79%, are concerned about environmental impacts,
including electricity use, water consumption, and greenhouse gas
emissions.

Looking Ahead
As Canada expands its AI capabilities, public support appears to
depend not only on innovation itself, but on how that innovation is
implemented. Canadians are looking for growth that balances
economic opportunity with affordability, environmental
responsibility, and transparent investment decisions.
For governments, utilities, and technology companies, building
trust may prove just as important as building the infrastructure.
Methodology
This study is based on an online survey of 1,505 Canadian
residents aged 18 and older, conducted from July 10 to 13,
2026, using Leger’s LEO online panel. Results were weighted
according to age, gender, mother tongue, region, education, and
presence of children in the household to ensure a representative
sample of the Canadian population. A margin of error cannot be
associated with a non-probability sample, but for comparison, a
probability sample of this size would have a margin of error of
±2.53%, 19 times out of 20.
