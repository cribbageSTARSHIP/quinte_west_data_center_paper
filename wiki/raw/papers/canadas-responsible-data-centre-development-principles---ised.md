Canada's Responsible Data Centre
Development Principles
23–29 minutes
Data centres are critical infrastructure for Canada. They support
the health care, financial services, research and secure
government systems Canadians rely on, while powering artificial
intelligence and creating new opportunities for communities and
businesses. These opportunities must be pursued responsibly,
with careful stewardship of electricity and water and meaningful
involvement of host communities. These principles provide a
common national framework for responsible data centre
development.
1. Data centres must create lasting local beneﬁts
Data centre investments must create clear, lasting beneﬁts for host
communities and Indigenous rights holders. These may include
jobs, skills training and apprenticeships, local and Indigenous
procurement, tax contributions, infrastructure investment, research
partnerships and access to compute for local businesses and
institutions. Proponents must engage early with host communities
and Indigenous rights holders to identify beneﬁts that reﬂect their
priorities.

2. Data centres must not shift electricity costs to
Canadians
Data centre growth must not shift project-driven electricity system
costs onto households and existing businesses. Proponents must
pay the costs of connecting and serving their projects, including
new generation, transmission, substations and grid upgrades
directly attributable to their development, proportionate to their
scale and impact. Projects must not compromise grid reliability
and, where required, must contribute new supply, infrastructure,
storage or demand ﬂexibility.
3. Data centres must minimize water use and
environmental impacts
Data centres must minimize freshwater consumption and protect
local water supplies, taking account of local conditions, resource
constraints and overall environmental performance. Priority should
be given to sustainable, low-impact technologies and practices,
including closed-loop or high efficiency water technologies, waste
heat recovery, and low-emission energy sources. Water use and
other environmental impacts must be measured and transparently
reported using recognized standards.
4. Data centres must be transparent about local
impacts
Proponents must engage early and transparently with impacted
local governments and Indigenous communities on data centre
projects and respect local approval processes. They must provide
clear, project-appropriate and independently veriﬁable information

5. Data centres must bring strategic value to
Canada
Data centre investments must create clear and lasting beneﬁts for
Canada, demonstrated through outcomes such as investment, tax
revenue, Canadian supply-chain participation, innovation, research
partnerships, workforce development and access to compute for
Canadian businesses and institutions. Projects should strengthen
Canada's long-term digital resilience, security and compute
capacity. Facilities supporting sensitive data, public services, and
critical infrastructure and systems must meet appropriate
requirements for Canadian legal protection, cybersecurity,
continuity of service and reliable Canadian access.
