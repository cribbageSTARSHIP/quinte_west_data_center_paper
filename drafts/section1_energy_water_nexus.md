# Section I: Resource Extraction, Grid Instability, and Watershed Depletion

## 1.1 US Grid Projections and Regional Capacity Constraints

The US grid faces significant strain, with projections indicating a 12% increase in demand by 2028 (^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 3]). Alberta, a key energy-producing region, experiences a notable disparity between power load requests (20,000 MW) and generation capacity (10,000 MW), exacerbating grid instability (^[Source: California data center health impacts tripled in 4 years.pdf, Page 4]). Ontario's Independent Electricity System Operator (IESO) further highlights capacity constraints, as highlighted in comparative analyses of power generation infrastructure (^[Source: /opt/data/wiki/concepts/power-grid-strain-in-alberta.md]).

## 1.2 Evaporative Cooling, Aquifer Strain, and Generation-Water Linkages

Evaporative cooling in power generation consumes significant water resources, measured in litres per million gallons per day (MGD). This process, critical for thermal power plants, places immense pressure on local watersheds, particularly in arid regions (^[Source: /opt/data/wiki/concepts/water-consumption-in-ai-data-centers.md]). Aquifer strain is directly correlated with generation load, as increased power demand escalates water consumption for cooling, leading to unsustainable depletion of groundwater reserves (^[Source: /opt/data/wiki/concepts/water-consumption-from-power-generation.md]).

## 1.3 Comparative Analysis: Canada vs US Metrics

While both Canada and the US face challenges in balancing energy demand and water use, Canada's policies on water consumption for data centers and power generation demonstrate a more regulated approach (^[Source: /opt/data/wiki/concepts/canadas-water-policies-for-data-centers.md]). However, the US, particularly in regions like Virginia, exhibits higher water scarcity impacts due to increased data center energy demands and environmental stressors (^[Source: /opt/data/wiki/concepts/virginia-data-center-energy-demand-and-environmental-impact.md] and [Source: /opt/data/wiki/concepts/water-scarcity-and-health-impacts-in-virginia.md]). These contrasts underscore the need for region-specific strategies in managing the energy-water nexus.
