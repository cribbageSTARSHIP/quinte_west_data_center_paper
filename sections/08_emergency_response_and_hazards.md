## Section 8: Emergency Response Protocols and Secondary Municipal Hazards

### 8.1: External Regulatory and Emergency Agencies Called for Chemical Spills in Quinte West Beyond Police, Fire, and EMS

The Ontario Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks (MECP) Spills Action Centre is the primary provincial authority for reporting and managing spills of pollutants, including hazardous substances. Under provincial law, individuals or entities responsible for spills must report to the Spills Action Centre by telephone, as outlined in [Source: Ontario Spills Action Centre (MECP) | https://www.ontario.ca/page/report-pollution-and-spills].

In addition to the MECP, the Lower Trent Conservation Authority may be involved in spills affecting local water bodies. CANUTEC (Canadian Transport Emergency Centre), operated by Transport Canada, provides emergency response guidance for spills involving dangerous goods, as detailed in [Source: CANUTEC Emergency Response | https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods/canutec].

The Technical Standards and Safety Authority (TSSA) oversees the safe operation of pressure vessels and other equipment that could contribute to spills. Hastings Prince Edward Public Health may be involved in spills with potential public health impacts, and Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) would be engaged for spills with broader environmental significance.

### 8.2: Contamination Vectors Posed by Data Centers

Data centers pose a range of environmental contamination risks, including:

- **Diesel Fuel Hydrocarbons:** Used in backup generators, diesel fuel leaks can contaminate soil and groundwater, as seen in the 2026 Secaucus data center spill, where 5,000+ gallons of diesel entered local wetlands ^[Source: Patch Secaucus | https://patch.com/new-jersey/secaucus/data-center-spills-5-500-gallons-diesel-secaucus-creek].

- **Inhibited Glycol:** Used in cooling systems, glycol leaks can contaminate waterways and soil.

- **BESS Lithium-Ion Thermal Runaway:** Battery energy storage systems (BESS) can release toxic gases like hydrogen fluoride (HF) and phosphoryl fluoride during thermal runaway events, as described in [Source: Wikipedia Reference - Thermal runaway | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_runaway].

- **PFAS Fire-Suppression Runoff:** Fire suppression systems using per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) can leave persistent contaminants in the environment.

- **Transformer Dielectric Oils:** Electrical transformers use oils that can leak into the environment during failures.

### 8.3: Documented Data Center Contamination Mechanisms in Canada

In Canada, documented contamination incidents include:

- **Quebec (Urgence-Environnement & St. Lawrence Basin):** Scrutiny of cooling fluid purges and biocide discharges into storm networks during system flushing in Beauharnois and Montreal.

- **Alberta (AUC & AEP Regulatory Filings):** Administrative reviews of bulk fuel containment for multi-engine diesel arrays in Olds and the Calgary corridor, with concerns over NO2 and particulate matter exceedances.

- **Cross-Border Precedent:** The 2026 Secaucus data center diesel spill is cited in Canadian public hearings as a case study for backup fuel transfer risks.

### 8.4: Documented Data Center Contamination Mechanisms Worldwide

Internationally, notable cases include:

- **Equinix NY2 Bulk Diesel Spill (Secaucus, NJ):** A malfunction in the fuel management system led to a 5,500-gallon diesel spill into Anderson Creek, prompting coordinated emergency response by local authorities and Clean Harbors ^[Source: PCMag | https://www.pcmag.com/news/data-center-spills-thousands-of-gallons-of-diesel-into-nj-creek-after-computer].

- **Northern Virginia Data Center Alley:** Aggregate diesel volumes from over 10,000 standby generators pose air quality risks, leading to a $2.4M Supplemental Environmental Project (SEP) enforcement order by the Virginia DEQ.

### 8.5: Canadian Government Regulatory Enforcement and Accident Response Protocols

In Canada, regulatory enforcement includes:

- **Ontario MECP Spills Action Centre:** Mandatory spill reporting and immediate containment protocols.

- **CANUTEC:** Emergency response guidance for dangerous goods spills, with a 24-hour hotline for incidents.

- **Alberta's Regulatory Framework:** Enforces compliance with emissions standards and requires environmental impact assessments for new data centers.

- **ECCC:** Oversees national environmental standards and coordinates with provincial agencies for cross-border incidents.

### 8.6: International Government Regulatory Enforcement and Accident Response Protocols

Internationally, regulatory frameworks include:

- **United States EPA Guidance:** The Clean Air Act regulates emissions from data center stationary engines and backup generators, with specific guidelines for emergency operations ^[Source: Clean Air Act Resources for Data Centers US EPA.pdf, Page 2].

- **California Data Center Regulations:** Emphasize sustainable energy use and emissions reductions, with studies showing health impacts from data center pollutants ^[Source: California data center health impacts tripled in 4 years.pdf, Page 2].

- **European Union Directives:** Implement strict environmental impact assessments and emissions controls for data centers.
