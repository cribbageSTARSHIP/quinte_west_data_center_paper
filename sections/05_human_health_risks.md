## Section 5: Human Health Risks (Physical and Mental)

### 5.1: Physical Health Impacts of Data Centers

Data centers contribute to physical health risks through multiple pathways:

- **Particulate Matter (PM2.5):** Exposure to PM2.5, a microscopic particulate pollutant, is associated with respiratory and cardiovascular diseases. Studies indicate that prolonged inhalation of PM2.5 can lead to lung damage, exacerbate asthma, and increase the risk of ischemic heart disease [^[Source: Wikipedia Reference - Particulates | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter]]].

- **Nitrogen Oxides (NOx) and Diesel Exhaust:** Nitrogen oxides, particularly NOx, are linked to respiratory issues and are known carcinogens. Diesel exhaust emissions from data center generators contribute to air pollution and have been associated with hypertension and increased risk of cardiovascular diseases [^[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger their local communities and the country as a whole.pdf, Page 2]]].

- **Asthma and Respiratory Conditions:** The particulate and gaseous emissions from data centers have been correlated with asthma exacerbations in nearby communities, particularly in vulnerable populations such as children and the elderly [^[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger their local communities and the country as a whole.pdf, Page 3]]].

### 5.2: Mental Health Impacts of Data Centers

The operational characteristics of data centers can adversely affect mental health in surrounding communities:

- **Chronic Low-Frequency Noise Annoyance:** Continuous low-frequency noise from cooling systems and generators has been shown to elevate nocturnal cortisol levels, leading to chronic stress and insomnia [^[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger their local communities and the country as a whole.pdf, Page 5]]].

- **Cognitive Fatigue and Environmental Distress:** Prolonged exposure to the persistent humming sound from data centers can lead to cognitive fatigue, reduced concentration, and increased environmental distress in local communities [^[Source: 5 ways data centers endanger their local communities and the country as a whole.pdf, Page 8]]].

### 5.3: Canadian and International Legislation Addressing Data Center Health Risks

Governments worldwide have introduced legislation to mitigate the health risks posed by data centers:

- **Ontario Ambient Air Quality Criteria (AAQC):** The AAQC sets limits on air pollutants to protect residents from harmful exposure, including PM2.5 and NOx emissions [^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 5]]].

- **Environmental Compliance Approvals (ECA) under EPA Section 9:** In Canada, data centers must obtain ECAs to ensure they comply with air quality and noise regulations, including adherence to ambient air quality standards [^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 4]]].

- **US Clean Air Act Title V Permits:** In the United States, data centers are required to obtain Title V permits, which impose strict emissions controls and air quality monitoring requirements to prevent adverse health impacts [^[Source: A moratorium on Ai data centres Canadians.pdf, Page 3]]].

- **WHO Night Noise Guidelines:** The World Health Organization provides evidence-based noise exposure limits to mitigate the adverse mental health impacts of noise pollution, which are increasingly relevant in data center-dense regions [^[Source: Wikipedia Reference - Health effects from noise | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_from_noise]]].
