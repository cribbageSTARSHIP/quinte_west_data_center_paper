# Potential Power Draw from a Data Center at 920 Trenton Frankford Road: A Comprehensive Analysis  


![Figure 1.7: Parcel Boundary and Substation Footprint Map — 920 Trenton-Frankford Road](wiki/raw/maps/quinte_west_Sonoco_data_center.svg){width=90%}

## Introduction  

The proposed data center at **920 Trenton Frankford Road** in Quinte West, Ontario, represents a significant intersection of municipal infrastructure, energy policy, and environmental sustainability. This site, formerly occupied by the **Sonoco Paper Mill**, is now under consideration for redevelopment by **FirstBlock Inc.**, a Quebec-based company specializing in high-efficiency data centers and waste heat recovery systems ^[12]. The project aligns with broader provincial and municipal goals for decarbonization, digital sovereignty, and economic revitalization. However, the potential power draw from such a facility necessitates a rigorous analysis of existing infrastructure, regulatory frameworks, and environmental implications. This paper examines the technical, legal, and ecological dimensions of the proposed data center, with a focus on its energy requirements, grid integration, and implications for the **Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed**, **Quinte West**, and **Hastings County**.  

---

## 1. Geospatial and Industrial Context of 920 Trenton Frankford Road  

### 1.1 Site History and Zoning  

The **920 Trenton Frankford Road** site is located within the **Quinte West Municipal Boundary**, a region characterized by a mix of industrial, residential, and ecological zones. The site’s historical use as a **Sonoco Paper Mill** is documented in municipal geospatial records, which also highlight its proximity to the **Trent River** and its connection to the **Bay of Quinte watershed** ^[1]. The Trent River, a critical ecological corridor, flows through Quinte West and into the Bay of Quinte, a large freshwater estuary that supports diverse aquatic ecosystems and recreational activities.  

The **Saputo Redevelopment Site** at **7 Riverside Drive**—a former dairy facility—serves as a comparative case study for industrial repurposing in the region. Both sites are subject to **municipal industrial zoning regulations** that permit hyperscale and edge data center proposals, provided they meet environmental and infrastructure standards ^[1]. The 920 Trenton Frankford Road site, however, is uniquely positioned to leverage existing electrical infrastructure, including **44 kV industrial feeders** from the **Hydro One Sidney Transformer Station** ^[1].  

### 1.2 Infrastructure Legacy and Grid Capacity  

The **Sonoco Paper Mill** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road was previously licensed by the **Ontario Energy Board (OEB)** for electricity generation, though its primary function was industrial rather than data center operations. The **Electricity Generation Licence EG-2010-0168** granted to **Sonoco Canada Corporation** included a **7.5 MW facility** at **5 Bernard Long Road, Trenton**, which may have shared grid connections with the 920 Trenton Frankford Road site ^[4]. This legacy infrastructure could facilitate the integration of a new data center, though the **power draw** of such a facility would depend on its scale, cooling systems, and energy efficiency metrics.  

The **Quinte West municipal grid** has faced challenges in the past, including a **2022 power outage** linked to a **transformer failure** at the **Harder Drive substation**, managed by **Elexicon Energy** ^[7]. This incident exposed vulnerabilities in substation redundancy and emergency response protocols, raising questions about the capacity of the existing grid to support high-demand facilities like data centers.

## 2. Power Draw Estimation and Grid Integration  

### 2.1 Typical Power Requirements for Data Centers  

Data centers are among the most energy-intensive facilities in the modern economy, with power consumption ranging from **10 to 100 MW** depending on scale, cooling technology, and computational demands. The **FirstBlock Inc.** project at 920 Trenton Frankford Road emphasizes **high-efficiency liquid cooling** and **waste heat recovery**, which could reduce energy consumption compared to traditional air-cooled data centers ^[12]. However, even with these innovations, the facility is likely to draw significant power from the grid.  

A **2026 study** on low-frequency noise from chillers and substations notes that **hyperscale cooling systems** can generate **structural resonance** and **vibroacoustic coupling**, which may necessitate additional electrical infrastructure to mitigate these effects ^[6]. This implies that the power draw could be higher than conventional estimates due to the need for redundant cooling systems.  

### 2.2 Grid Capacity and Hydro One Infrastructure  

The **Hydro One Sidney Transformer Station** provides **44 kV feeders** to the 920 Trenton Frankford Road site, a critical component of the **Quinte West power grid** ^[1]. However, the **capacity of these feeders** and the **existing load on the grid** must be assessed to determine whether the site can support a new data center without overloading the network.  

The **Ontario Energy Board’s** licensing of **Sonoco Canada Corporation** included obligations to **maintain system integrity** and comply with **Market Rules** for electricity generation ^[10]. These rules may apply to the new data center as well, though the **Ontario Energy Board Act, 1998** does not explicitly address data center operations. This regulatory gap could pose challenges for grid integration and load management.  

### 2.3 Comparative Analysis: Saputo vs. FirstBlock  

The **Saputo Redevelopment Site** at **7 Riverside Drive** is another industrial site in Quinte West that has been proposed for a **TrueNorth/Bell AI data center** ^[1]. This project, like FirstBlock’s, would require significant power draw, but the **Saputo site** is located along the **Trent River**, raising concerns about **thermal discharge** and **water usage** for cooling. In contrast, the **FirstBlock project** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road incorporates **waste heat recovery**, which could reduce the need for **freshwater intake** and mitigate ecological impacts on the **Bay of Quinte** ^[12].

## 3. Legal and Regulatory Framework  

### 3.1 Ontario Energy Board Licensing  

The **Ontario Energy Board (OEB)** has historically licensed **electricity generation facilities**, including the **Sonoco Paper Mill** at 5 Bernard Long Road, Trenton ^[4]. However, **data centers** are not explicitly covered under the **Ontario Energy Board Act, 1998**, which focuses on **generation, transmission, and distribution** of electricity. This regulatory ambiguity could complicate the approval process for the **FirstBlock project**, as the **Ontario Energy Board** may not have jurisdiction over data center operations.  

The **Electricity Generation Licence EG-2010-0168** granted to **Sonoco Canada Corporation** includes **obligations to comply with Market Rules** and **system integrity requirements** ^[10]. These rules may apply to the new data center if it is classified as an **energy consumer** rather than a **generator**, though this classification is not explicitly addressed in the **OEB Act**.  

### 3.2 Municipal Zoning and Environmental Regulations  

The **Quinte West Municipal Boundary** includes **industrial zoning** that permits **hyperscale data centers**, provided they meet **noise**, **emission**, and **water usage** standards ^[1]. The **FirstBlock project** must comply with **municipal noise bylaws**, which currently enforce **A-weighted decibel (dBA)** thresholds but fail to account for **low-frequency noise** from **chillers and substations** ^[6]. This could lead to **residential complaints** and **legal challenges** if the data center’s operations exceed permissible noise levels.  

The **Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed** is protected under provincial and federal **water quality regulations**, which limit **thermal discharge** and **chemical runoff** from industrial facilities ^[1]. The **FirstBlock project’s** use of **waste heat recovery** may help mitigate these risks, but **environmental impact assessments** (EIAs) would be required to confirm compliance.

## 4. Environmental and Health Considerations  

### 4.1 Low-Frequency Noise and Health Impacts  

The **low-frequency noise** (below 100 Hz) generated by **data center chillers** and **electrical substations** has been linked to **health risks**, including **elevated nighttime cortisol**, **cardiovascular strain**, and **sleep disturbance** ^[6]. The **Quinte West municipal noise bylaws**, which rely on **A-weighted decibel (dBA)** thresholds, do not account for **C-weighted (dBC)** noise peaks, which are more relevant to **structural resonance** and **vibroacoustic coupling** ^[6].  

The **FirstBlock project** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road would require **acoustic mitigation measures**, such as **noise barriers** or **substation shielding**, to comply with **municipal and provincial regulations**. Failure to address low-frequency noise could result in **legal disputes** and **community opposition**, similar to past incidents involving **industrial facilities** in the region.  

### 4.2 Water Usage and Thermal Discharge  

Data centers typically consume large volumes of **freshwater** for **cooling**, with **air-cooled systems** requiring **millions of liters per day**. The **Saputo Redevelopment Site** at 7 Riverside Drive, which is also under consideration for a data center, would likely rely on **air-cooling**, increasing **water usage** and **thermal discharge** into the **Trent River** ^[1].  

In contrast, the **FirstBlock project** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road incorporates **liquid cooling** and **waste heat recovery**, which could reduce **freshwater intake** and **thermal pollution** ^[12]. However, the **Bay of Quinte watershed** is already under pressure from **agricultural runoff** and **industrial discharges**, and any additional **thermal load** from the data center would need to be **monitored** and **mitigated**.

## 5. Conclusion and Recommendations  

The **potential power draw** from the **FirstBlock data center** at **920 Trenton Frankford Road** is a critical factor in its feasibility and environmental impact. While the site benefits from **existing grid infrastructure** and **municipal zoning**, the **high energy demands** of data centers, combined with **regulatory gaps** and **environmental risks**, necessitate a **comprehensive assessment**.  

Key recommendations include:  
1. **Grid Capacity Analysis**: Conduct a detailed assessment of the **Hydro One Sidney Transformer Station** and **Quinte West power grid** to ensure the site can support the data center’s power draw without overloading the network ^[1].  
2. **Noise Mitigation**: Implement **low-frequency noise control measures** to comply with **municipal and provincial regulations** and avoid **health risks** to nearby residents ^[6].  
3. **Water Usage Management**: Prioritize **waste heat recovery** and **closed-loop cooling systems** to minimize **freshwater consumption** and **thermal discharge** into the **Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed** ^[12].  
4. **Regulatory Clarification**: Advocate for **Ontario Energy Board** guidelines on **data center operations** to ensure **grid stability** and **compliance with Market Rules** ^[10].  

By addressing these challenges, the **FirstBlock project** can serve as a **model for sustainable data center development** in **Quinte West**, **Hastings County**, and beyond.
