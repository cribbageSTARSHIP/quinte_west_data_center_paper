# Historical Details of 920 Trenton Frankford Road (Former Sonoco Paper Mill Site): A Comprehensive Analysis


![Figure 1.7: Parcel Boundary and Substation Footprint Map — 920 Trenton-Frankford Road](wiki/raw/maps/quinte_west_Sonoco_data_center.svg){width=90%}

## Introduction  
The site at **920 Trenton Frankford Road** in Quinte West, Ontario, has undergone significant transformations since its inception as a paper mill. This paper provides an exhaustive historical and technical analysis of the site, tracing its evolution from an industrial hub under **Sonoco Canada Corporation** to its current proposed redevelopment as a **sustainable data center** by **FirstBlock Inc.** The analysis integrates municipal, environmental, and infrastructural contexts, including comparisons with adjacent sites such as the **former Saputo Dairy at 7 Riverside Drive** and the broader **Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed**.  

---

## Historical Context and Industrial Legacy  

### 1. Early Industrial Use and Sonoco’s Acquisition  
The **Sonoco Paper Mill** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road was established as a key industrial facility in Quinte West, leveraging the region’s proximity to the **Trent River** and its hydroelectric potential. By **2007**, **Sonoco Canada Corporation** acquired the site, acquiring the **Trenton generation facility** from **Toromont Energy Ltd.** and installing a **3.9 MW turbine** at the site ^[2]. This acquisition marked a pivotal moment in the site’s history, aligning Sonoco’s operations with the **Ontario Energy Board (OEB)** regulations and establishing a legal framework for electricity generation ^[12].  

The **electricity generation licence (EG-2010-0168)** granted to Sonoco in 2010 explicitly authorized the operation of a **7.5 MW facility** at the site, though the exact location of the generation infrastructure (whether at 920 Trenton Frankford Road or another Sonoco site) remains ambiguous in the source materials ^[12]. However, the **2012 OEB decision** to amend the licence confirmed Sonoco’s operational rights, citing an **implied contract with Hydro One Networks Inc.** for excess electricity sales ^[2].  

### 2. Decline of the Paper Mill Industry  
By the early 2010s, the paper mill industry in Quinte West faced challenges due to **global market shifts** and **environmental regulations**. The **closure of the Sonoco Paper Mill** by the mid-2010s left the site underutilized, prompting discussions about redevelopment. This transition is reflected in the **2026 FirstBlock Inc. proposal**, which reimagines the site as a **data center** with **waste heat recovery** ^[3].

## Environmental and Geospatial Context  

### 1. Watershed and Floodplain Analysis  
The site is located within the **Lower Trent Conservation watershed**, a critical ecological zone encompassing **2,070 square kilometers** of the **Trent River** and its tributaries ^[7]. The **Trent River** flows into the **Bay of Quinte**, forming a vital corridor for **aquatic biodiversity** and **recreational activities** such as fishing and boating ^[8].  

A **topographic survey** conducted by **IBW Surveyors** in May 2026 revealed that the site’s **lowest ground elevation** (91.12 m) is **0.56 m above the regulatory flood level** (90.56 m) ^[5]. This margin of safety is critical for infrastructure planning, particularly for the proposed **data center** and **heat recovery systems**. The **floodplain map** from **KGS Group / Lower Trent Conservation** (sheet 112, Feb. 2024) further confirms that the site is **not within a flood zone**, though the **Trent River’s floodplain** extends nearby ^[5].  

### 2. Municipal Zoning and Infrastructure  
Quinte West’s **industrial zoning** for **hyperscale data centers** and **edge computing** is outlined in the **Quinte West Municipal Boundary and Industrial Zoning** document ^[1]. The site at 920 Trenton Frankford Road is classified under **heavy industrial zoning**, aligning with its historical use and the proposed **FirstBlock redevelopment**.  

A **zoning confirmation** from the **City of Quinte West’s Director of Planning** (June 12, 2026) explicitly permits the **data center** and **heat recovery infrastructure** ^[4]. This contrasts with the **Saputo redevelopment site** at **7 Riverside Drive**, which is also under consideration for a **TrueNorth/Bell AI data center** (Planning File D09/T09/26) ^[1]. Both sites reflect Quinte West’s strategic push toward **green energy** and **digital infrastructure**, leveraging the **Trent River’s hydroelectric potential** and **existing power grids**.

## Current Redevelopment: FirstBlock’s Sustainable Data Center  

### 1. Project Overview and Technical Specifications  
FirstBlock Inc. proposes a **33 MW data center** at the former Sonoco Paper Mill site, divided into **Phase 1 (16 MW)** and **Phase 2 (17 MW)** ^[9]. The project aims to **recover waste heat** from servers and **distribute it via a thermal loop** to **greenhouses, farms, and neighboring buildings**, reducing reliance on **natural gas** for heating.  

Key technical features include:  
- **High-efficiency liquid cooling** and **heat recovery systems** (capable of reusing **100% of rejected heat** during peak periods) ^[9].  
- **Noise mitigation**: Independent acoustic studies by **HGC Engineering** (P.Eng.) confirm that noise levels at nearby homes will range from **34 to 41 dBA**, well below **Ontario’s NPC-300 limits** (50 dBA by day, 45 dBA at night) ^[10].  
- **Flood resilience**: The site’s **0.56 m elevation above flood level** ensures compliance with **municipal and provincial floodplain regulations** ^[5].  

### 2. Environmental and Economic Benefits  
The project is projected to **reduce GHG emissions** by **4,220 tonnes of CO₂ per year** in Phase 1 and **8,725 tonnes** in Phase 2, assuming **natural gas** would otherwise supply heat ^[9]. This aligns with **Ontario’s decarbonization goals** and **Quinte West’s commitment to **sustainable development**.  

Economically, the project is expected to **generate regional jobs** and **strengthen Canada’s digital sovereignty** through **high-performance computing (HPC) infrastructure** ^[3]. This contrasts with the **Saputo site**, which focuses on **AI and edge computing** but lacks the **heat recovery component** of FirstBlock’s proposal.

## Legal and Regulatory Framework  

### 1. Ontario Energy Board (OEB) Licensing  
Sonoco’s **electricity generation licence (EG-2010-0168)** was amended in **2012** to include the **Trenton generation facility** ^[2]. The **OEB decision** (April 17, 2012) emphasized that the facility’s operations were **dispatchable** under a contract with the **Ontario Power Authority** and **Hydro One Networks Inc.** ^[11]. This legal framework enabled Sonoco’s integration into **Ontario’s grid**, though the site’s **paper mill operations** were not explicitly tied to the **electricity generation** in the source materials.  

### 2. Municipal By-Laws and Zoning  
The **Interim Control By-law 26-131** (October 7, 2026) permits FirstBlock’s **data center** and **heat recovery infrastructure** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road ^[6]. This by-law reflects Quinte West’s **strategic alignment** with **net-zero goals** and **green energy initiatives**, contrasting with the **Saputo site**’s focus on **AI and edge computing** without **heat reuse**.

## Conclusion: Legacy and Future Trajectory  

The **920 Trenton Frankford Road** site exemplifies the **transformation of industrial legacies** into **sustainable infrastructure**. From its **Sonoco-era paper mill** and **hydroelectric operations** to its **current redevelopment** as a **zero-waste data center**, the site’s history is intertwined with **Quinte West’s economic and environmental policies**.  

The **Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed** provides a **critical ecological context**, ensuring that redevelopment projects like FirstBlock’s **heat recovery system** align with **floodplain safety** and **biodiversity preservation** ^[5][7]. As Quinte West continues to **attract data center investments** (e.g., Saputo at 7 Riverside Drive), the **Sonoco site’s** integration of **energy efficiency** and **community benefits** sets a **benchmark for sustainable redevelopment** in the region.
