## Historical Details of 7 Riverside Drive, Trenton: The Former Saputo Dairy Site

### Introduction  
The site at 7 Riverside Drive in Trenton, Ontario, has undergone significant transformations since its inception as a dairy processing facility. This paper provides an exhaustive analysis of the historical, environmental, and infrastructural context of the former Saputo Dairy site, situating it within the broader framework of Quinte West, the Trent River watershed, and the Bay of Quinte region. Drawing on primary sources, including municipal planning documents, environmental assessments, and public engagement records, this paper examines the site’s evolution from its industrial past to its proposed future as a data center, while addressing concerns raised by residents and stakeholders.

---

### Historical Context of the Saputo Dairy Site  

#### Early Industrial Use and Ownership  
The property at 7 Riverside Drive was originally developed as a dairy processing facility, a critical component of Trenton’s industrial economy during the 20th century. While specific dates of initial construction are not explicitly documented in the provided sources, the site’s association with Saputo Canada Inc., a major dairy producer, is well established. Saputo’s operations at this location were part of its broader network of facilities across Ontario, contributing to the region’s reputation as a hub for food processing and agricultural innovation.  

The closure of the Saputo dairy in 2020 marked a pivotal moment in the site’s history. According to source [3], the building has remained vacant since that time, presenting an opportunity for redevelopment. The closure was likely influenced by shifting market dynamics, including the consolidation of dairy operations and the increasing cost of compliance with environmental and labor regulations. This transition from active industrial use to vacancy set the stage for subsequent proposals for the site’s repurposing.  

#### Legal and Zoning Framework  
The property is legally described as *Part Lot 1 Concession 2 Part 5, 21R8907, Parts 1 And 3, 21R5254 And Parts 4 And 5 21R541 Except Part 2 & 4, 21R5254; S/T QR221514* in the former City of Trenton, now part of Quinte West ^[8]. Initially zoned under the Service Industrial (SM) category, the site underwent a rezoning application in 2026 to transition to the Service Industrial Exception 14 (SM-14) zone, which would permit the construction of a data center ^[8]. This change reflects the evolving economic priorities of the region, shifting from traditional manufacturing to high-tech infrastructure.

### Environmental and Infrastructure Context  

#### Water Resources and the Trent River Watershed  
The Trent River, a key tributary of the Bay of Quinte, flows through Trenton and Quinte West, forming a critical ecological and hydrological corridor. The Trent River watershed is part of the larger Lake Ontario–St. Lawrence River system, with significant implications for water quality, biodiversity, and municipal infrastructure. The Trenton Water Distribution System, which includes a treatment plant capable of processing 35,800 cubic meters of water per day, serves the region and is a focal point for infrastructure planning ^[2].  

The proposed data center at 7 Riverside Drive has raised concerns about potential impacts on water resources. Residents and environmental advocates have highlighted the risk of increased water usage for cooling systems, which could strain the existing distribution network ^[9]. This concern is particularly salient given the proximity of the site to the Trent River and the broader Bay of Quinte watershed, which is already under pressure from agricultural runoff, urban development, and climate change-induced variability in precipitation patterns.  

#### Power and Utility Infrastructure  
The site’s location on Riverside Drive places it within a network of power and utility corridors that are central to Quinte West’s infrastructure planning. Source [1] notes the importance of hyper-local power routing in the region, with Quinte Development’s utilities page outlining the challenges of integrating new industrial projects into existing grids ^[1]. The proposed data center would require significant electrical capacity, raising questions about the adequacy of current power infrastructure and the potential for blackouts or grid instability, as referenced in source [4] ^[4].  

The City of Quinte West has also been engaged in broader infrastructure improvements, including the planned construction of a new water tower and pump station near Mount Pelion ^[2]. While these projects are not directly related to 7 Riverside Drive, they underscore the region’s focus on modernizing utilities to meet the demands of both residential and industrial users.

### Rezoning and Redevelopment Proposals  

#### The Data Center Proposal  
In 2026, the City of Quinte West’s Planning Advisory Committee considered a rezoning application for 7 Riverside Drive, which would allow the site to be redeveloped as a data center. The proponent, TrueNorth Sustainable Infrastructure, proposed a $320 million micro data center, which would create 14 full-time jobs ^[3]. The application was deferred until after the October 2026 municipal election, with the newly elected council tasked with deciding its fate ^[7].  

The proposed data center would occupy the former Saputo building, which has been vacant since 2020. The rezoning application sought to transition the site from the Service Industrial (SM) zone to the Service Industrial Exception 14 (SM-14) zone, a classification that permits data center operations ^[8]. This change would align the site with Quinte West’s broader economic strategy to attract high-tech industries, as outlined in the *Discovery Guide* for the region ^[12].  

#### Economic and Environmental Trade-offs  
While the data center proposal promises economic benefits, including job creation and increased tax revenue, it has also sparked significant opposition. Residents raised concerns about noise pollution, excessive electricity consumption, and the potential strain on water resources ^[9]. These concerns are not unique to 7 Riverside Drive but reflect broader tensions between industrial development and environmental sustainability in the Bay of Quinte region.  

The environmental impact assessment for the project, though not detailed in the provided sources, would need to address the following:  
- **Energy Consumption**: Data centers are known for their high energy demands, which could exacerbate existing challenges in Quinte West’s power grid.  
- **Water Usage**: Cooling systems in data centers often require large volumes of water, potentially conflicting with the needs of the Trent River watershed and local communities.  
- **Noise and Light Pollution**: The proximity of the site to residential areas and natural trails (e.g., the Great Lakes Waterfront Trail) raises concerns about the impact on quality of life and ecological integrity ^[5].

### Conclusion  

The historical trajectory of 7 Riverside Drive, Trenton, reflects the broader economic and environmental dynamics of the Quinte West region. From its origins as a Saputo dairy facility to its current status as a vacant site under consideration for a data center, the property has been shaped by shifting industrial priorities, municipal planning, and community engagement. The proposed redevelopment underscores the tension between economic growth and environmental stewardship, particularly in the context of the Trent River watershed and the Bay of Quinte’s ecological significance.  

As Quinte West moves forward with its infrastructure and economic development plans, the case of 7 Riverside Drive serves as a microcosm of the challenges and opportunities facing communities in the transition to a low-carbon, technology-driven economy. The lessons learned from this site will be critical for future projects, ensuring that development aligns with both economic aspirations and environmental imperatives.
