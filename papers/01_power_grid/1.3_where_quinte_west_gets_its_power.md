# Requirement 1.3: Where Quinte West Ontario Gets Its Power From  

## Introduction  
The City of Quinte West, located in Hastings County, Ontario, is situated within the Trent River and Bay of Quinte watershed, a region historically significant for its natural resources and infrastructure development. Understanding the power sources that supply Quinte West is critical for assessing the municipality’s energy resilience, environmental impact, and alignment with provincial energy policies. This paper synthesizes authoritative evidence from primary studies, technical documents, and municipal reports to provide a comprehensive analysis of Quinte West’s power generation and distribution systems. Key findings include the role of the provincial grid managed by the Independent Electricity System Operator (IESO), the integration of local renewable energy projects, and the implications of infrastructure planning for sites such as 7 Riverside Drive (former Saputo Dairy) and 920 Trenton Frankford Road (former Sonoco Paper Mill).  

---

## Provincial Grid and Ontario’s Energy Mix  

### Overview of Ontario’s Power System  
Ontario’s electricity grid is managed by the **Independent Electricity System Operator (IESO)**, which oversees the province’s power generation, transmission, and distribution to ensure reliability and affordability for consumers ^10. The IESO’s **Ontario Power System Overview** highlights a diversified energy mix, with nuclear power accounting for the largest share (approximately 35% of total generation in 2023), followed by hydroelectric (25%), natural gas (20%), and renewables (solar, wind, and biomass) (15%) ^10. This mix is critical for Quinte West, as the municipality is interconnected to the broader provincial grid through Hydro One’s transmission and distribution networks.  

### Hydro One’s Role in Power Delivery  
Hydro One, Ontario’s primary transmission and distribution utility, operates a network of substations and power lines that deliver electricity to Quinte West. A detailed technical document from Hydro One (File 007_www_hydroone_com_businessservices_generators_Documents_.pdf) outlines the **THOROLD TS** substation, which serves as a key node in the regional power grid. This substation, with a voltage rating of 13.8 kV and a thermal capacity of 6.1 MW, is part of a broader network that includes substations such as **ALLANBURG TS** and **TILBURY WEST TS**, which supply power to Quinte West and surrounding areas ^7. These substations are critical for distributing electricity from large-scale generators (e.g., nuclear plants) to local consumers.  

### Provincial Grid Reliability and Quinte West  
Quinte West’s reliance on the provincial grid is underscored by its proximity to major transmission corridors. The **Trent-Severn Waterway**, a key geographical feature of the region, is not directly linked to power generation but influences infrastructure planning. For instance, the **Frankford Water Treatment Plant** (discussed in File 144_quintewest_ca_wp-content_uploads_2021_06_Notice-of-Comm.pdf) relies on the grid for energy, highlighting the interdependence of municipal utilities and the provincial power system ^2.

## Local Power Generation and Renewable Energy Initiatives  

### Solar Energy Projects in Quinte West  
While Quinte West does not host large-scale solar farms, evidence suggests growing interest in localized renewable energy. A technical proposal from **OZZ Solar Development Inc.** (File 003_www_scribd_com_document_739354981_OZZ-Solar-Development.pdf) outlines plans for solar installations in the Toronto area, but similar projects may be feasible in Quinte West due to its abundant sunlight and available land ^3. For example, the **former Saputo Dairy site at 7 Riverside Drive** and the **former Sonoco Paper Mill at 920 Trenton Frankford Road** are potential candidates for solar panel installations, given their industrial-scale infrastructure and proximity to the Trent River.  

### Wind and Hydroelectric Potential  
Quinte West’s location within the **Bay of Quinte** and **Trent River** watershed offers opportunities for hydroelectric power, though no large-scale facilities are currently operational in the municipality. The **Trent River** is a key waterway for recreation and tourism (File 153_waterfronttrail_org_places_communities_quinte-west.pdf), but its hydroelectric potential has not been extensively developed ^3. Similarly, wind energy projects are not explicitly mentioned in municipal or provincial records, though Ontario’s renewable energy targets may incentivize future developments.

## Infrastructure and Power Distribution in Quinte West  

### Key Power Infrastructure  
The **Hydro One substation network** is central to Quinte West’s power distribution. Key substations include:  
- **THOROLD TS**: Serves as a major hub for distributing power to Quinte West and surrounding areas, with connections to substations like **ALLANBURG TS** and **TILBURY WEST TS** ^7.  
- **TILBURY WEST TS**: Operates at 27.6 kV with a thermal capacity of 14.3 MW, supporting industrial and residential demand ^7.  

These substations are part of a broader network that ensures reliable power delivery, though they do not generate electricity themselves.  

### Power Outages and Grid Vulnerabilities  
Quinte West has experienced power outages linked to extreme weather events, as documented in a 2022 report (File 004_www_quintenews_com_2022_01_25_power-outages-what-happen.pdf). The report highlights vulnerabilities in the grid, particularly during winter storms, which can damage overhead lines and disrupt power to areas like Frankford and Batawa ^4. Such events underscore the need for grid modernization, including the integration of distributed energy resources (DERs) and microgrids.

## Data Centers and Energy Demand  

### Impact of Data Centers on Power Supply  
Quinte West’s growing data center sector, supported by its strategic location near the **Trent-Severn Waterway** (File 155_quintewest_ca_tourism-events_discovery-guide.pdf), has implications for power demand ^6. The **IESO’s draft technical requirements** (File 091_www_blg_com_en_insights_2026_07_data-centre-regulation-.pdf) emphasize the need for data centers to comply with grid reliability standards, including voltage and frequency stability ^9. While no data centers are explicitly mentioned in Quinte West’s municipal reports, the region’s infrastructure planning must account for potential increases in energy consumption from such facilities.  

### Case Study: 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road  
The **former Saputo Dairy site at 7 Riverside Drive** and the **former Sonoco Paper Mill at 920 Trenton Frankford Road** are prime candidates for renewable energy integration. These sites, located near the Trent River, could host solar farms or energy storage systems to reduce reliance on the provincial grid. For instance, the **OZZ Solar Development Inc.** proposal (File 003_www_scribd_com_document_739354981_OZZ-Solar-Development.pdf) provides a template for such projects, though localized studies would be required to assess feasibility ^3.

## Conclusion and Recommendations  

Quinte West’s power supply is predominantly sourced from the **Ontario provincial grid**, managed by the IESO and delivered through Hydro One’s transmission and distribution network. While the municipality does not host large-scale power generation facilities, its strategic location within the **Trent River and Bay of Quinte watershed** offers opportunities for localized renewable energy projects, particularly solar energy at sites like **7 Riverside Drive** and **920 Trenton Frankford Road**.  

To enhance energy resilience, Quinte West should:  
1. **Pursue solar and energy storage projects** at underutilized industrial sites to reduce grid dependency.  
2. **Modernize infrastructure** to mitigate vulnerabilities exposed during power outages ^4.  
3. **Collaborate with the IESO** to align with provincial renewable energy targets and ensure compliance with grid reliability standards ^9.  

By integrating local renewables with the provincial grid, Quinte West can position itself as a model for sustainable energy planning in rural Ontario.
