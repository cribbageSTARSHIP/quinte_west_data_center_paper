# Where Quinte West Gets Its Drinking Water From: A Comprehensive Analysis of Sources, Infrastructure, and Protection Measures  

## Introduction  

The City of Quinte West, located in Hastings County, Ontario, relies on a dual-source drinking water system that draws from the Trent River and the Bay of Quinte. This system is critical to the region’s public health, environmental sustainability, and municipal infrastructure resilience. The water supply is managed through two primary treatment plants: the Trenton Water Treatment Plant (Trenton Plant) and the Bayside Water Treatment Plant (Bayside Plant), each serving distinct geographic areas and populations. The Trent River and Bay of Quinte watersheds are subject to rigorous protection under the **Ontario Clean Water Act** (2006) and the **Trent Source Protection Plan** (2015), which aim to mitigate contamination risks from industrial, agricultural, and urban activities. This paper provides an exhaustive analysis of Quinte West’s drinking water sources, treatment processes, regulatory frameworks, and the implications of proposed developments at two key sites: **7 Riverside Drive** (former Saputo Dairy) and **920 Trenton Frankford Road** (former Sonoco Paper Mill).

## 1. Drinking Water Sources: The Trent River and Bay of Quinte  

### 1.1 The Trent River Watershed  

The **Trent River** is the primary source of drinking water for the Trenton Service Area of Quinte West. The river flows through the **Trent River watershed**, which spans five Conservation Authority areas: Lower Trent, Crowe Valley, Ganaraska Region, Kawartha Region, and Otonabee. This watershed is divided into **Intake Protection Zones (IPZ-1, IPZ-2, IPZ-3)** based on vulnerability scores derived from proximity to water intakes, contaminant travel time, and intake depth ^[Source 2]. IPZ-1, the most vulnerable zone, is located closest to the Trenton Water Treatment Plant’s intakes, while IPZ-3 is further downstream and less vulnerable ^[Source 2].  

The **Lower Trent Conservation (LTC)** oversees the Trent River watershed, enforcing the **Clean Water Act** to map vulnerable areas, regulate industrial activities, and monitor groundwater quality in **Wellhead Protection Areas (WHPA)** ^[Source 3]. Key conservation reserves, such as **Bleasdell Boulder** (a glacial landform critical for groundwater recharge) and **Sager Conservation Area** (protecting riparian zones), are integral to maintaining the integrity of the Trent River’s water supply ^[Source 3].  

### 1.2 The Bay of Quinte  

The **Bay of Quinte**, a part of Lake Ontario, serves as the primary source for the **Bayside Service Area** of Quinte West. The Bayside Water Treatment Plant draws water from the Bay through a **404-meter-long intake pipe** extending 370 meters into the Bay ^[Source 5]. This conventional, chemically-assisted filtration plant has a rated capacity of **11,360 cubic meters per day (cu.m/day)**, with a maximum flow rate of **262.8 liters per second (L/s)** ^[Source 4]. The **Permit to Take Water (PTTW)** for this system, **P-300-9303346859**, expires on **December 6, 2034** and allows the City to withdraw up to **11,365 cu.m/day** ^[Source 4].  

The Bay of Quinte is also subject to **Source Protection Plan** requirements, with **Risk Management Plans (RMPs)** reviewed by the **Risk Management Official** for developments within the **Intake Protection Zone (IPZ)** surrounding the Bayside intake ^[Source 4]. In 2024, three applications were submitted for review, though no RMPs were required for the properties identified ^[Source 4].

## 2. Water Treatment Infrastructure: Trenton and Bayside Plants  

### 2.1 Trenton Water Treatment Plant  

The **Trenton Water Treatment Plant** draws water from the **Trent River upstream of Dam No. 1** through two intake pipes:  
- A **53-meter-long, 400 mm diameter** raw water intake pipe.  
- A **18-meter-long, 600 mm diameter** intake pipe ^[Source 8].  

The plant has a **rated capacity of 35,800 cu.m/day**, with a **maximum flow rate of 530.4 L/s** ^[Source 7]. The **Permit to Take Water (PTTW)** for this system, **1007-9JHP6L**, expires on **April 30, 2024** ^[Source 7].  

**Treatment Processes**:  
- **Flocculation** and **sedimentation** to remove suspended particles.  
- **Granular Activated Carbon (GAC) Filtration** to adsorb organic contaminants.  
- **Chlorine gas** is used for disinfection, followed by storage in **two interconnected baffled clearwells** with a combined capacity of **5,454 cu.m** ^[Source 8].  

### 2.2 Bayside Water Treatment Plant  

The **Bayside Water Treatment Plant** draws water from the **Bay of Quinte** through a **404-meter-long intake pipe** extending 370 meters into the Bay ^[Source 5]. The plant employs the following treatment processes:  
- **Rapid-mix coagulation** and **flocculation** to remove turbidity.  
- **Sedimentation** and **dual-media filtration** (sand and anthracite).  
- **Granular Activated Adsorber (GAA) cells** for additional contaminant removal.  
- **Sodium hypochlorite** for disinfection, with **fluoridation** of the drinking water supply ^[Source 5].  

The treated water is pumped to the **Water Distribution System (WDS)** via **two vertical turbine high-lift pumps** ^[Source 5].

## 3. Regulatory Framework and Source Protection Measures  

### 3.1 The Ontario Clean Water Act and Source Protection Plans  

The **Ontario Clean Water Act** (2006) mandates the protection of drinking water sources through **Source Protection Plans (SPPs)**, which identify vulnerable areas and implement risk management strategies ^[Source 1]. The **Trent Source Protection Plan** (2015) specifically targets the Trent River and Bay of Quinte watersheds, requiring **Risk Management Plans (RMPs)** for developments within **Intake Protection Zones (IPZs)** ^[Source 2].  

### 3.2 Intake Protection Zones (IPZs) and Risk Management  

The **IPZs** are categorized based on vulnerability:  
- **IPZ-1**: Closest to intakes, with the highest risk of contamination.  
- **IPZ-2**: Moderate risk, located further from intakes.  
- **IPZ-3**: Least vulnerable, downstream of intakes ^[Source 2].  

For the **Trenton Plant**, **three RMPs** were required in 2021 for properties within the **Trenton IPZ**, with draft plans distributed to affected property owners ^[Source 7]. In contrast, the **Bayside IPZ** required no RMPs in 2024, though applications were reviewed for compliance with the **Source Protection Plan** ^[Source 4].  

### 3.3 Enforcement and Monitoring  

The **Lower Trent Conservation (LTC)** enforces the **Clean Water Act** by:  
1. **Mapping vulnerable areas** (IPZ-1, IPZ-2, IPZ-3).  
2. **Regulating industrial activities** near intakes (e.g., hazardous waste storage permits).  
3. **Monitoring groundwater quality** in **Wellhead Protection Areas (WHPA)** ^[Source 3].  

The **City of Quinte West** also conducts **microbiological testing** and reports spills to the **Spills Action Centre** as required under the **Safe Drinking Water Act** ^[Source 6].

## 4. Proposed Developments and Their Implications  

### 4.1 7 Riverside Drive (Former Saputo Dairy)  

The **former Saputo Dairy site** at **7 Riverside Drive** is located within the **Trent River watershed**, potentially within the **Trenton IPZ**. Any redevelopment here would require compliance with the **Source Protection Plan**, including submission of a **Risk Management Plan (RMP)** to the **Risk Management Official** ^[Source 7]. The site’s proximity to the **Trenton Water Treatment Plant** necessitates rigorous environmental assessments to prevent contamination risks from industrial activities, such as chemical spills or improper waste storage ^[Source 2].  

### 4.2 920 Trenton Frankford Road (Former Sonoco Paper Mill)  

The **former Sonoco Paper Mill site** at **920 Trenton Frankford Road** is situated in the **Frankford area**, which is part of the **Trent River watershed**. This site may fall within the **Trenton IPZ** or **Frankford IPZ**, depending on its proximity to water intakes. Redevelopment here would require adherence to the **Source Protection Plan**, including **RMPs** for hazardous waste storage or chemical use ^[Source 2]. The **Lower Trent Conservation (LTC)** would likely enforce strict regulations to mitigate risks to the **Trent River’s water quality** ^[Source 3].

## 6. Conclusion and Recommendations  

Quinte West’s drinking water supply is a critical component of the region’s infrastructure, relying on the **Trent River** and **Bay of Quinte** for its dual-source system. The **Trenton and Bayside Water Treatment Plants** employ advanced filtration and disinfection processes to ensure water safety, while the **Ontario Clean Water Act** and **Trent Source Protection Plan** provide legal and regulatory frameworks to mitigate contamination risks.  

Proposed developments at **7 Riverside Drive** and **920 Trenton Frankford Road** must adhere to **Source Protection Plan** requirements, including **Risk Management Plans (RMPs)** and **hazardous waste storage permits**. The **Lower Trent Conservation (LTC)** and **City of Quinte West** must continue enforcing stringent monitoring and compliance measures to safeguard the **Trent River** and **Bay of Quinte** watersheds. Future infrastructure projects should prioritize **environmental impact assessments** and **sustainable development practices** to ensure the long-term viability of Quinte West’s drinking water supply.
