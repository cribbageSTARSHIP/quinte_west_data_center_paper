# Requirement 7.4: Trades and Job Titles Required to Run a Data Center Long Term  

## Introduction  
Data centers are critical infrastructure for modern digital economies, requiring a complex interplay of technical, environmental, and regulatory expertise to operate efficiently and sustainably. As highlighted in recent studies, data centers consume vast amounts of energy and water, with cooling systems alone accounting for significant portions of their environmental footprint (Alston et al., 2023; Gour et al., 2026). The long-term operation of a data center necessitates a multidisciplinary workforce capable of managing energy systems, environmental impacts, legal compliance, and infrastructure maintenance. This paper synthesizes authoritative evidence from academic and industry sources to outline the trades and job titles essential for sustaining data center operations, with specific relevance to the City of Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed. The proposed sites at 7 Riverside Drive (former Saputo Dairy) and 920 Trenton Frankford Road (former Sonoco Paper Mill) require tailored expertise to address local water resource constraints, energy demands, and regulatory frameworks.  

---

## Technical and Operational Trades  

### 1. **HVAC and Cooling System Technicians**  
Data centers rely on advanced cooling systems to manage heat generated by servers, with configurations ranging from water-cooled chillers to air-cooled economizers (Alston et al., 2023). The hybrid cooling approach, which balances energy efficiency and water consumption, requires specialized technicians to monitor and maintain systems. Key roles include:  
- **HVAC Technicians**: Responsible for installing, maintaining, and repairing heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems. They must understand the intricacies of airside economizers, waterside economizers, and chiller systems (Source [1], lines 701-800).  
- **Cooling System Engineers**: Design and optimize cooling infrastructure, ensuring compliance with energy efficiency standards and minimizing water usage (Source [3], Table 2).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The Trent River watershed’s water availability must be carefully managed, as data centers using water-cooled systems could strain local resources. Technicians must ensure that cooling systems align with regional water conservation policies.  

---

### 2. **Electrical and Power Systems Technicians**  
Data centers require robust electrical infrastructure to power servers, cooling systems, and backup generators. Key roles include:  
- **Electrical Technicians**: Install and maintain high-voltage electrical systems, including uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) and transformers.  
- **Power Distribution Engineers**: Design and oversee the distribution of electricity, ensuring redundancy and compliance with safety standards (Source [6], Section 3.1.1).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The proposed sites must integrate with the local grid, which includes transmission corridors with thermal limits of 6,000 MW (Source [6], lines 501-600). Power engineers will need to coordinate with utility providers to avoid congestion and ensure reliable energy supply.  

---

### 3. **IT and Network Engineers**  
Data centers house vast computing resources, requiring expertise in network infrastructure and server management:  
- **Network Engineers**: Configure and maintain high-speed data networks, ensuring low latency and high bandwidth for cloud services.  
- **Server Administrators**: Oversee the deployment, monitoring, and maintenance of server hardware and software (Source [11], Section 10).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: As a hub for emerging data center development, Quinte West must ensure that IT infrastructure supports both local and regional digital needs, including potential AI training workloads (Source [4], lines 501-600).  

---

## Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Roles  

### 4. **Legal and Tax Compliance Officers**  
Data center projects involve complex legal and financial considerations, including tax incentives and regulatory approvals:  
- **Tax Counsel**: Assess federal and provincial incentives, such as Canada’s Clean Technology Investment Tax Credit (30% of capital costs) (Source [11], Section 9).  
- **Regulatory Compliance Officers**: Ensure adherence to environmental laws, such as those governing water usage and emissions (Source [2], Page 3).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The City of Quinte West must navigate provincial economic development programs and infrastructure incentives to support data center growth while complying with environmental regulations (Source [2], Page 3).  

---

### 5. **Environmental and Sustainability Officers**  
Data centers have significant environmental impacts, particularly in water consumption and energy use. Roles include:  
- **Environmental Scientists**: Monitor water usage, wastewater discharge, and carbon footprints, ensuring compliance with local and federal standards (Source [12], Page 4).  
- **Sustainability Officers**: Develop strategies to reduce water consumption, such as adopting hybrid cooling systems (Source [1], lines 701-800).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed faces pressure from increasing water demands. Sustainability officers must implement measures to minimize the data center’s water footprint, such as using glycol-based cooling systems with minimal contamination risks (Source [5], Page 5).  

---

## Maintenance and Infrastructure Trades  

### 6. **Glycol System Specialists**  
Cooling systems often use glycol-based fluids for freeze protection, requiring specialized maintenance:  
- **Glycol System Technicians**: Monitor and maintain glycol concentrations, ensuring proper system performance and preventing contamination (Source [5], Page 5).  
- **Chemical Engineers**: Develop inhibited glycol blends to extend fluid life and protect infrastructure (Source [9], Page 2).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The proposed sites must address risks of glycol contamination in cooling loops, which could impact local water quality if not properly managed (Source [5], Page 5).  

---

### 7. **HVAC and Plumbing Technicians**  
Data centers require extensive plumbing systems for cooling and wastewater management:  
- **Plumbing Technicians**: Install and maintain piping systems for water-cooled chillers and wastewater treatment.  
- **HVAC Technicians**: Ensure proper integration of cooling systems with building infrastructure (Source [10], Page 1).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The former Saputo Dairy site (7 Riverside Drive) may require retrofitting existing plumbing systems to meet data center cooling demands, necessitating skilled technicians (Source [5], Page 5).  

---

## Safety and Emergency Response Roles  

### 8. **Health and Safety Officers**  
Data centers must prioritize worker and community safety, particularly in high-risk areas like chemical handling and electrical systems:  
- **Occupational Health and Safety Officers**: Develop protocols for managing glycol contamination, electrical hazards, and emergency shutdown procedures (Source [10], Page 1).  
- **Emergency Response Teams**: Train for scenarios such as fire suppression, chemical spills, and power outages (Source [12], Page 4).  

**Relevance to Quinte West**: The Trent River’s proximity to the proposed sites necessitates strict safety protocols to prevent chemical spills from affecting aquatic ecosystems (Source [10], Page 1).

## Conclusion  

The long-term operation of a data center requires a multidisciplinary workforce spanning technical, legal, environmental, and safety domains. In Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed, these roles must be tailored to address local water resource constraints, energy grid limitations, and environmental protection mandates. The proposed sites at 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road will need specialized expertise in hybrid cooling systems, glycol contamination prevention, and compliance with provincial incentives to ensure sustainable and efficient operations. As data centers continue to expand, the integration of these trades will be critical to balancing technological advancement with ecological and community well-being.
