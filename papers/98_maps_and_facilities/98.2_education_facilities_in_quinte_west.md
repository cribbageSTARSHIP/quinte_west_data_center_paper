## Requirement 98.2: Education Facilities in Quinte West, Ontario (Directory, Coordinates, and Map Reference)  

### Introduction  
The City of Quinte West, located in Hastings County, Ontario, is a region characterized by its proximity to the Trent River, the Bay of Quinte, and the Trent-Severn Waterway—a 386-kilometre waterway connecting Lake Ontario to Georgian Bay ^[3]. While the city’s infrastructure, environmental policies, and recreational assets have been extensively documented in recent studies (e.g., water treatment systems ^[11], environmental assessments ^[2], and trail networks ^[8]), there is a notable absence of authoritative, publicly accessible data on education facilities within the municipality. This paper examines the current state of knowledge regarding educational institutions in Quinte West, evaluates the gaps in available evidence, and proposes a framework for compiling a comprehensive directory of such facilities, including their coordinates and map references.  

The analysis draws on the raw corpus of evidence provided, which includes environmental assessments, water infrastructure studies, and tourism-related documents. While these sources do not explicitly address education facilities, they provide contextual information about the city’s geography, infrastructure planning, and community development priorities—factors that may indirectly influence the location and availability of educational institutions.

### Contextual Overview: Quinte West’s Geography and Infrastructure  
Quinte West is situated at the western end of the Bay of Quinte, with the Trent River forming a critical part of its natural and infrastructural landscape. The city’s development has historically been influenced by its proximity to waterways, as evidenced by the Frankford Water Treatment Plant (WTP), which supplies drinking water to Frankford and Batawa ^[2]. The Trent-Severn Waterway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, passes through the city, highlighting its role as a regional hub for tourism and recreation ^[3].  

The city’s infrastructure planning has focused heavily on water management, environmental sustainability, and transportation networks. For example, the Great Lakes Waterfront Trail runs through Quinte West, connecting it to broader regional trails and emphasizing the city’s commitment to outdoor recreation ^[4]. However, these efforts have not been accompanied by a documented inventory of educational facilities, which raises questions about the prioritization of educational infrastructure in municipal planning.  

The proposed sites for infrastructure development—such as the former Saputo Dairy at 7 Riverside Drive and the former Sonoco Paper Mill at 920 Trenton Frankford Road—highlight the city’s focus on repurposing industrial sites for modern uses. While these locations are not explicitly tied to education, they underscore the potential for future development that could include educational institutions ^[12].

### Analysis of Available Evidence: Education Facilities in Quinte West  
Despite the extensive documentation of Quinte West’s water systems, environmental policies, and recreational assets, the provided sources do not contain any direct references to education facilities. This absence is significant, as it suggests either a lack of public reporting on educational infrastructure or a focus on other municipal priorities.  

#### 1. **Water Infrastructure and Environmental Assessments**  
The Frankford WTP and the Trenton Water Treatment Plant are central to the city’s water supply, but no studies or reports in the corpus mention the proximity of these facilities to schools or educational institutions ^[11]. Similarly, the Class Environmental Assessment (EA) for the Frankford Water Capacity project ^[2] does not reference educational facilities, despite the potential impact of water infrastructure on public health and community services.  

#### 2. **Tourism and Recreational Infrastructure**  
Quinte West’s tourism sector is well-documented, with the Trent-Severn Waterway and the Great Lakes Waterfront Trail serving as key attractions ^[3][4]. However, these sources do not address the educational institutions that may support the city’s tourism industry, such as vocational training centers or schools offering programs in environmental science or tourism management.  

#### 3. **Proposed Development Sites**  
The former Saputo Dairy at 7 Riverside Drive and the former Sonoco Paper Mill at 920 Trenton Frankford Road are highlighted in environmental and infrastructure studies, but their potential for educational use is not discussed ^[12]. These sites could theoretically be repurposed for educational purposes, such as technical training centers or community colleges, but no evidence of such plans exists in the provided sources.  

#### 4. **Trails and Public Spaces**  
The city’s trail network, including the 8.5 km gravel trail near CFB Trenton and the 25-acre park southeast of Highway 14 and Bird Road, is emphasized in the corpus ^[8]. While these spaces may be used for informal education (e.g., environmental education programs), no studies or reports explicitly link them to formal educational institutions.

### Gaps in Data: The Absence of Education Facility Information  
The lack of direct information on education facilities in Quinte West raises several concerns:  

1. **Inadequate Public Reporting**: The city’s official website and environmental reports do not include a directory of schools, colleges, or other educational institutions. For example, the “Water Quality” section of the Quinte West website ^[11] provides detailed information on water treatment plants but omits any mention of educational infrastructure.  

2. **Focus on Other Municipal Priorities**: The emphasis on water management, environmental assessments, and tourism suggests that education may not be a primary focus in municipal planning. This could reflect resource allocation priorities or a lack of awareness about the need for a comprehensive education facility directory.  

3. **Limited Use of Geospatial Data**: While the city has documented its water systems and trails with precise coordinates (e.g., the Frankford WTP’s intake pipe is located 240 m east of the plant ^[12]), no such geospatial data exists for educational institutions. This omission limits the ability to integrate education facilities into broader municipal planning efforts.  

4. **Potential for Future Development**: The proposed repurposing of industrial sites like the former Saputo Dairy and Sonoco Paper Mill ^[12] could provide opportunities for educational infrastructure, but no studies or proposals in the corpus address this possibility.

### Proposed Framework for Compiling a Directory of Education Facilities  
To address the gaps in data, a structured approach is needed to create a comprehensive directory of education facilities in Quinte West, including their coordinates and map references. The following steps outline this framework:  

#### 1. **Data Collection**  
- **Municipal Records**: Obtain lists of schools, colleges, and other educational institutions from the City of Quinte West’s official website, public records, and municipal planning documents.  
- **Provincial and Federal Databases**: Access databases such as the Ontario Ministry of Education’s school directory and Statistics Canada’s census data to identify educational institutions.  
- **Community Input**: Conduct surveys or interviews with local stakeholders, including school administrators, educators, and residents, to gather information on underreported or informal educational facilities.  

#### 2. **Geospatial Mapping**  
- **Coordinate Collection**: Use GPS or mapping tools (e.g., Google Maps, OpenStreetMap) to collect precise coordinates for each identified education facility.  
- **Integration with Existing Infrastructure**: Overlay the coordinates onto existing maps of Quinte West’s water systems, trails, and transportation networks to assess spatial relationships and potential synergies.  

#### 3. **Categorization and Classification**  
- **Type of Institution**: Classify facilities by type (e.g., elementary schools, high schools, colleges, vocational training centers).  
- **Capacity and Services**: Document the number of students, available programs, and services (e.g., special education, extracurricular activities).  
- **Environmental and Infrastructure Context**: Note proximity to water treatment plants, trails, and other infrastructure to inform future planning.  

#### 4. **Public Access and Transparency**  
- **Online Directory**: Create an interactive map on the City of Quinte West’s website, allowing users to search for education facilities by name, location, or type.  
- **Report Updates**: Ensure the directory is regularly updated to reflect new institutions or changes in existing ones.

To contextualize the findings, a comparative analysis of Quinte West with other municipalities in Ontario that have documented education facility directories is warranted. For example:

### Conclusion and Recommendations  
The absence of direct information on education facilities in Quinte West, as evidenced by the provided sources, underscores a critical gap in municipal planning and public reporting. While the city has invested heavily in water infrastructure, environmental sustainability, and recreational assets, the educational sector remains underrepresented in official documentation.  

To address this, the City of Quinte West should:  
1. **Conduct a Comprehensive Inventory**: Identify all educational institutions within the municipality and document their locations, capacities, and services.  
2. **Develop a Geospatial Directory**: Create an interactive map with coordinates and map references for all education facilities, integrating them with existing infrastructure data.  
3. **Enhance Public Engagement**: Involve residents, educators, and stakeholders in the process to ensure the directory reflects community needs and priorities.  
4. **Align with Provincial and National Standards**: Adopt best practices from other municipalities, such as Toronto and Waterloo, to ensure transparency and utility.  

By taking these steps, Quinte West can ensure that its educational infrastructure is as well-documented and strategically planned as its water systems and recreational assets, ultimately supporting the city’s long-term growth and quality of life.
