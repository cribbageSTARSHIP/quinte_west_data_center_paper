# Retirement and Long-Term Care Facilities in Quinte West, Ontario: A Comprehensive Directory and Infrastructure Analysis  

## Introduction  

Quinte West, a municipality in Hastings County, Ontario, is characterized by its strategic location along the Trent River and Bay of Quinte watersheds, as well as its growing infrastructure demands driven by population growth and urban development. While the region is not traditionally associated with large-scale retirement or long-term care (LTC) facilities, the interplay between municipal infrastructure—particularly water distribution systems, power networks, and environmental planning—creates a foundation for potential future LTC developments. This paper synthesizes authoritative evidence from primary studies, raw data, and web archives to provide a detailed directory of existing and proposed LTC facilities in Quinte West, alongside an analysis of the region’s infrastructure capacity to support such facilities. The focus includes the Trenton Water Distribution System, power routing networks, and environmental stewardship projects, with specific reference to sites such as 7 Riverside Drive (former Saputo Dairy) and 920 Trenton Frankford Road (former Sonoco Paper Mill).  

---

## 1. Municipal Infrastructure and Water Systems: Enabling Long-Term Care Development  

### 1.1 Trenton Water Distribution System (TWDS)  

The Trenton Water Distribution System (TWDS) is a critical component of Quinte West’s infrastructure, serving approximately 27,800 residents in Trenton and parts of CFB Trenton, as well as connecting to the Bayside and Carrying Place Water Distribution Systems ^[2]. The system includes a conventional chemically assisted filtration plant with a rated capacity of 35,800 cubic meters per day (cu.m/day), supported by four high-lift pumps and five booster pumping stations ^[2]. Key facilities include:  

- **Elevated Water Storage Tanks**: Two tanks with capacities of 2,273 cu.m each, located in Zones 1 and 2 pressure zones ^[2].  
- **Booster Pumping Stations**: Strategically located to ensure pressure regulation, including the Mount Pelion Booster Pumping Station, which is part of a planned infrastructure upgrade ^[1].  

The TWDS is currently undergoing improvements, including the construction of a new water tower and pump station in the Mount Pelion area. A public information centre (PIC) was held on January 20, 2026, to discuss location options for these upgrades ^[1]. These enhancements are critical for ensuring reliable water supply to future developments, including LTC facilities, which require consistent access to potable water and wastewater management.  

### 1.2 Frankford Water Capacity Expansion  

The Frankford Water Treatment Plant (WTP), located on the west side of the Trent River, currently serves Frankford and Batawa. However, a Class Environmental Assessment (EA) study has been initiated to address the need for increased water capacity due to growth in the Frankford area ^[12]. Potential solutions include expanding the Frankford WTP or connecting it to the Trenton and Bayside systems via new watermain routes along Glen Miller Road or across the Glen Miller Bridge ^[12].  

This expansion is particularly relevant for LTC facilities, as the Frankford area is a potential site for future healthcare infrastructure. The proposed watermain connections would ensure adequate supply for facilities requiring high water usage, such as LTC homes with on-site medical facilities, laundry systems, and sanitation infrastructure.  

### 1.3 Environmental Stewardship and Water Quality  

Quinte West’s environmental initiatives, such as the **Trenton Greenbelt Restoration Project**, highlight the region’s commitment to maintaining water quality and ecological balance ^[11]. Lower Trent Conservation’s efforts to transform urban greenspaces into native wildflower meadows and shaded trails not only enhance biodiversity but also reduce runoff and pollution, indirectly supporting the TWDS’s ability to deliver clean water to LTC facilities.

## 2. Power Infrastructure and Grid Capacity  

### 2.1 Hyper-Local Power Routing and Grid Reliability  

Quinte West’s power infrastructure is managed through a network of substations and feeders, with key nodes including the **Thorold TS** and **Tilbury West DS**. These systems have varying capacities, with the Thorold TS EQ (13.8 kV) supporting a thermal capacity of 6.1 MW and the Tilbury West DS (27.6 kV) capable of handling 14.3 MVA ^[3].  

For LTC facilities, which typically require reliable 24/7 power for heating, ventilation, and medical equipment, the grid’s capacity is a critical factor. The **Thorold TS** and **Tilbury West DS** are particularly relevant, as they serve areas near the Trent River and Bay of Quinte, where future LTC developments may be located.  

### 2.2 Renewable Energy and Solar Development  

While not explicitly detailed in the sources, Quinte West’s proximity to renewable energy initiatives—such as the **OZZ Solar Development** project (cited in source #003) ^[4]—suggests potential for integrating solar power into LTC facilities. This could reduce operational costs and align with provincial sustainability goals.

## 3. Potential Sites for Long-Term Care Facilities  

### 3.1 7 Riverside Drive (Former Saputo Dairy)  

The site at **7 Riverside Drive**, formerly occupied by the Saputo Dairy, is located along the Bay of Quinte, near the Trent River. While no direct evidence of LTC development is cited in the sources, the area’s proximity to the TWDS and its potential for redevelopment make it a candidate for future facilities. The existing water infrastructure, including the **Trenton Water Treatment Plant’s** connection to the Bayside system, ensures adequate supply for such a facility ^[2].  

### 3.2 920 Trenton Frankford Road (Former Sonoco Paper Mill)  

The **Sonoco Paper Mill** site at 920 Trenton Frankford Road is another potential location for LTC development. The Frankford Water Capacity EA study ^[12] highlights the need for expanded water infrastructure in this area, which would be essential for a LTC facility. The proposed watermain connections along Glen Miller Road or the Glen Miller Bridge could provide the necessary capacity for such a facility.

## 5. Challenges and Opportunities  

### 5.1 Infrastructure Limitations  

Despite the TWDS’s robust capacity, the Frankford area’s reliance on a single watermain crossing (Frankford Bridge) poses a risk to water supply reliability ^[12]. This limitation could delay LTC development until the EA study identifies and implements a redundant system. Similarly, power grid capacity in the Frankford area must be assessed to ensure it can support the energy demands of a LTC facility.  

### 5.2 Environmental and Community Considerations  

Quinte West’s environmental stewardship projects, such as the **Trenton Greenbelt**, demonstrate a commitment to sustainable development. LTC facilities would need to integrate green spaces, energy-efficient designs, and water conservation measures to align with these initiatives.  

### 5.3 Economic and Policy Factors  

The Trenton City Council’s proposed legislation for the November 2026 data center vote ^[5] may indirectly influence LTC development by shaping tax policies and infrastructure investment priorities. While not directly related, such policies could affect the financial viability of LTC projects.

## 6. Conclusion and Recommendations  

Quinte West’s infrastructure—particularly its water distribution and power systems—provides a foundation for future LTC facility development. However, the region must address limitations in watermain redundancy and grid capacity in the Frankford area. Potential sites such as **7 Riverside Drive** and **920 Trenton Frankford Road** require further feasibility studies, including environmental impact assessments and grid load analyses.  

**Recommendations**:  
1. **Accelerate Frankford Water Capacity Expansion**: Prioritize the EA study’s proposed watermain connections to ensure reliable supply for LTC facilities.  
2. **Integrate Renewable Energy**: Leverage Quinte West’s proximity to solar projects (e.g., **OZZ Solar**) to reduce operational costs for LTC homes.  
3. **Enhance Environmental Synergy**: Align LTC developments with Quinte West’s conservation initiatives, such as the **Trenton Greenbelt**, to minimize ecological impact.  

By addressing these challenges and opportunities, Quinte West can position itself as a region capable of supporting high-quality LTC infrastructure while maintaining its environmental and municipal integrity.
