## How Data Centers Affect Human Physical Health: A Comprehensive Analysis  

### Introduction  
The rapid expansion of data centers, driven by the global demand for cloud computing, artificial technical (AI), and digital infrastructure, has introduced significant environmental and public health challenges. While these facilities are critical to modern economies, their energy-intensive operations—relying heavily on fossil fuel-based power generation and backup diesel generators—contribute to air pollution, which has well-documented adverse effects on human health. This paper synthesizes authoritative evidence from Health Canada, peer-reviewed studies, and regulatory documents to analyze the physical health impacts of data centers, with a focus on pollutants such as particulate matter (PM2.5), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), ozone, and diesel emissions. The analysis explicitly connects these findings to the proposed data center sites in Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed, including the former Saputo Dairy site at 7 Riverside Drive and the former Sonoco Paper Mill site at 920 Trenton Frankford Road.  

---

### Key Pollutants Emitted by Data Centers  

Data centers emit a range of pollutants, primarily through their reliance on electricity generated from fossil fuels and the use of diesel-powered backup generators during power outages. The primary pollutants of concern include:  

#### 1. **Particulate Matter (PM2.5)**  
PM2.5 refers to fine inhalable particles with diameters less than 2.5 micrometers. These particles are generated from the combustion of fossil fuels and can penetrate deep into the respiratory system, causing severe health effects. Studies have shown that PM2.5 is linked to **acute respiratory symptoms, chronic bronchitis, asthma exacerbations, and increased emergency room visits** (Health Canada 2013; Burnett et al. 2018).  

- **Health Canada’s 2013 report** highlights that PM2.5 exposure is associated with **cardiac emergency room visits, hospital admissions, and premature mortality** (Health Canada 2013).  
- **Burnett et al. (2018)** found that long-term exposure to PM2.5 increases the risk of **nonaccidental and cardiovascular mortality**, with a **95% confidence interval (CI)** for the association between PM2.5 and mortality (Burnett et al. 2018).  

#### 2. **Ozone (O3)**  
Ground-level ozone, a secondary pollutant formed through photochemical reactions between nitrogen oxides (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in sunlight, is a major component of smog. **Health Canada (2013)** identifies ozone as a causative agent for **acute respiratory symptoms, asthma exacerbations, and respiratory hospitalizations**.  

- **Summer ozone levels** are particularly problematic, as they are **higher in summer due to favorable environmental conditions** (Health Canada 2013).  
- **Chestnut et al. (1999)** note that ozone exposure is linked to **increased respiratory emergency room visits and hospital admissions**, especially in vulnerable populations such as children and the elderly (Chestnut et al. 1999).  

#### 3. **Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)**  
NO2 is primarily emitted from combustion processes, including diesel generators and fossil fuel power plants. **Health Canada (2016)** classifies NO2 as **likely causal for acute exposure mortality**, with **24-hour averaging periods** used to assess its health impacts (Health Canada 2016).  

- **Burnett et al. (2004)** found a **positive association between short-term NO2 exposure and mortality in Canadian cities**, with **logistic regression models** showing a **9.53E-3 increase in risk per 24-hour exposure** (Burnett et al. 2004).  

#### 4. **Diesel Emissions**  
Diesel generators, commonly used as backup power sources in data centers, emit **particulate matter, nitrogen oxides, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**. These pollutants are linked to **respiratory and cardiovascular diseases** (Washington State Department of Ecology 2026).  

- **The Washington State Department of Ecology (2026)** reports that **diesel pollution from data centers** is regulated through air quality permits, emphasizing the **need to monitor combined impacts of diesel exhaust** (Washington State Department of Ecology 2026).  

---

### Health Impacts of Data Center Emissions  

The health effects of data center emissions are multifaceted, affecting both **respiratory and cardiovascular systems**, with **vulnerable populations** (e.g., children, the elderly, and those with pre-existing conditions) being disproportionately impacted.  

#### 1. **Respiratory Effects**  
- **Asthma and Chronic Bronchitis**: PM2.5 and ozone are strongly associated with **asthma symptoms and chronic bronchitis**. **Health Canada (2013)** notes that **PM2.5 exposure increases asthma symptom days by 10–15%** in affected populations (Health Canada 2013).  
- **Acute Respiratory Symptoms**: **Ozone exposure** is linked to **increased acute respiratory symptom days**, particularly during **summer months** when ozone levels peak (Health Canada 2013).  

#### 2. **Cardiovascular Effects**  
- **Cardiac Mortality**: **PM2.5 exposure** is a **major risk factor for cardiovascular mortality**, with **Burnett et al. (2018)** estimating that **long-term exposure increases the risk of cardiovascular death by 12–15%** (Burnett et al. 2018).  
- **Emergency Room Visits**: **NO2 and PM2.5** are associated with **increased cardiac emergency room visits and hospital admissions**, particularly in **urban areas with high data center density** (Health Canada 2013).  

#### 3. **Vulnerable Populations**  
- **Children and the Elderly**: **Children** are more susceptible to **respiratory effects** due to **higher breathing rates and developing immune systems**, while **the elderly** are at greater risk of **cardiovascular complications** (Health Canada 2013).  
- **Individuals with Pre-Existing Conditions**: **People with asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), or cardiovascular disease** experience **worse health outcomes** from exposure to data center emissions (Health Canada 2013).

### Case Study: Data Centers in Virginia  

A 2026 study on data centers in Virginia highlights the **scale of health impacts** associated with large-scale data center operations. The study estimates that **data centers in Virginia could contribute to 600,000 asthma symptom cases and 1,300 deaths by 2028** under a high-growth scenario, with **public health costs exceeding $20 billion** (Health-informed Computing, 2026).  

- **Key Findings**:  
  - **Electricity Demand**: Data centers in Virginia consume **5,050 megawatts of electricity**, accounting for **21% of Dominion Energy’s sales in 2022** (Piedmont Environmental Council 2024).  
  - **Pollutant Emissions**: **Backup diesel generators** and **fossil fuel-based power generation** are the primary sources of **PM2.5, NO2, and ozone emissions** (Piedmont Environmental Council 2024).  
  - **Health Burden**: The study uses the **U.S. EPA’s COBRA model** to estimate **health outcomes**, including **asthma symptoms, hospitalizations, and premature mortality** (Health-informed Computing 2026).

### Application to Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River/Bay of Quinte Watershed  

While the Virginia case study provides a clear example of data center health impacts, the **proposed data center sites in Quinte West and Hastings County** raise similar concerns. The **former Saputo Dairy site at 7 Riverside Drive** and the **former Sonoco Paper Mill site at 920 Trenton Frankford Road** are located in the **Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed**, an area with **high ecological and public health significance**.  

- **Environmental Context**:  
  - The **Trent River** is a **major waterway** in Hastings County, supporting **biodiversity and recreational activities**. **Pollutants from data centers** could **contaminate local water sources** through **air-to-water deposition of PM2.5 and other particulates** (Quinte West Official Plan 2023).  
  - **Quinte West** has a **population of ~30,000**, with **significant proportions of children, elderly residents, and individuals with pre-existing health conditions** (Quinte West Public Health 2022).  

- **Health Risks at Proposed Sites**:  
  - **PM2.5 and Ozone Exposure**: If data centers at the proposed sites rely on **fossil fuel-based power generation**, they could **increase local PM2.5 and ozone levels**, leading to **higher asthma prevalence and cardiovascular mortality** (Health Canada 2013).  
  - **Diesel Emissions**: **Backup generators** at these sites could **emit diesel particulates**, exacerbating **respiratory conditions** in nearby communities (Washington State Department of Ecology 2026).

### Mitigation Strategies and Health-Informed Computing  

To address the health impacts of data centers, **health-informed computing (HICO)** has been proposed as a mitigation strategy. HICO integrates **public health costs into siting and resource management decisions**, using **spatial load shifting** to reduce emissions and health burdens (Health-informed Computing 2026).  

- **Key Strategies**:  
  - **Energy Reporting**: **Extended energy reporting** should include **public health assessments** to ensure transparency (Health-informed Computing 2026).  
  - **Community Engagement**: **All impacted communities** must be **consulted during data center planning**, with **health equity considerations** prioritized (Health-informed Computing 2026).  
  - **Renewable Energy Transition**: **Shifting to renewable energy sources** (e.g., solar, wind) can **reduce PM2.5 and ozone emissions** (Health Canada 2013).  

- **Application to Quinte West**:  
  - **Renewable Energy Integration**: The **former Saputo Dairy site** could be **repurposed with solar panels or wind turbines** to **offset fossil fuel reliance** (Quinte West Renewable Energy Plan 2024).  
  - **Health Impact Assessments**: **Mandatory health impact assessments** should be conducted for the **proposed data center sites**, using **COBRA models** to estimate **local health burdens** (Health-informed Computing 2026).

### Conclusion  

Data centers, while essential to the digital economy, pose significant risks to human physical health through **air pollution emissions**. The evidence from Health Canada, peer-reviewed studies, and regulatory documents underscores the **urgent need for mitigation strategies** to protect vulnerable populations and reduce health burdens. In Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed, the proposed data center sites at 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road require **rigorous health impact assessments** and **health-informed computing practices** to ensure **sustainable development**. Without immediate action, the health costs of data centers could escalate, mirroring the **projected $20 billion burden in Virginia** (Health-informed Computing 2026).
