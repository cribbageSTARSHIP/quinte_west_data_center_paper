# How Hydrocarbon-Based Generators from Data Centers Affect the Local Environment: A Case Study of Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River/Bay of Quinte Watershed  

## Introduction  

The proliferation of data centers, driven by the global demand for cloud computing, artificial technical, and digital infrastructure, has led to an increased reliance on hydrocarbon-based generators for backup power. These generators, typically fueled by diesel, are critical for maintaining "five nines" (99.999% uptime) in data center operations, ensuring continuous service even during grid outages ^12^. However, the environmental implications of such reliance are profound, particularly in regions like Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed, where ecological sensitivity and population density intersect with proposed data center developments. This paper examines the environmental impacts of hydrocarbon-based generators from data centers, drawing on authoritative evidence from Canadian air pollutant emissions inventories, health risk assessments, and case studies of diesel generator usage in critical infrastructure.  

## Emissions Profile of Hydrocarbon-Based Generators  

Hydrocarbon-based generators, particularly those powered by diesel, emit a range of pollutants, including nitrogen oxides (NOX), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), fine particulate matter (PM2.5), and black carbon. These pollutants are well-documented in Canada’s Air Pollutant Emissions Inventory (2026), which highlights the sectoral contributions of various industries to national emissions ^5^. While the oil and gas industry remains the largest contributor to NOX emissions (37% of total national emissions in 2024) ^5^, the role of data centers and their backup generators is increasingly relevant, especially in regions with high data center density.  

### Key Pollutants and Their Sources  

1. **Nitrogen Oxides (NOX)**:  
   - NOX emissions are primarily generated through high-temperature combustion processes, such as those in diesel engines. In 2024, the oil and gas industry accounted for 37% of total NOX emissions in Canada, with a 30% increase between 1990 and 2024 ^5^. While data centers are not explicitly listed as a major source in national inventories, their diesel generators could contribute to localized NOX spikes, particularly during backup power activation.  

2. **Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)**:  
   - VOCs are emitted during the combustion of hydrocarbons and are a precursor to ground-level ozone formation. The decline in VOC emissions since 2014 has been attributed to reduced venting from crude oil production and improved fugitive emission controls ^8^. However, diesel generators in data centers may offset these reductions if not equipped with advanced emission control technologies.  

3. **Fine Particulate Matter (PM2.5)**:  
   - PM2.5 emissions from diesel combustion are a significant concern due to their ability to penetrate deep into the respiratory system. In 2024, PM2.5 emissions increased by 6 kt (4%) in Quebec, 1 kt (40%) in Yukon, and 5 kt (154%) in the Northwest Territories and Nunavut combined ^4^. While these increases are attributed to industrial and transportation activities, the localized impact of data center generators in regions like Quinte West could exacerbate PM2.5 concentrations.  

4. **Black Carbon**:  
   - Black carbon, a component of PM2.5, is emitted through incomplete combustion and has a potent warming effect on the climate. Alberta, Saskatchewan, Ontario, and Quebec accounted for the majority of black carbon emissions in 2024, with transportation and off-road equipment being the primary sources ^2^. Diesel generators in data centers, if not optimized for combustion efficiency, could contribute to black carbon emissions in localized areas.  

## Environmental and Health Impacts  

The environmental and health impacts of hydrocarbon-based generator emissions are well-documented in scientific literature and public health assessments. Exposure to pollutants such as NOX, VOCs, PM2.5, and black carbon is linked to respiratory diseases, cardiovascular issues, and premature mortality ^3^.  

### Health Risks and Vulnerable Populations  

- **Respiratory and Cardiovascular Effects**:  
  Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) are strongly associated with increased hospital admissions for asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and heart failure. In Canada, extensive studies have shown that PM2.5 and NO2 are key contributors to smog formation, which disproportionately affects children, the elderly, and individuals with preexisting health conditions ^3^.  

- **Climate Forcing**:  
  Black carbon emissions from diesel generators contribute to climate change by absorbing solar radiation and reducing albedo (reflectivity) of snow and ice. In regions like the Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed, where ecological systems are sensitive to temperature changes, such emissions could accelerate local climate impacts ^2^.  

### Ecological Impacts  

- **Aquatic Ecosystems**:  
  The Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed is a critical habitat for fish species, including the endangered Lake Sturgeon. Airborne pollutants such as NOX and VOCs can deposit into water bodies, leading to eutrophication and acidification, which harm aquatic life ^3^.  

- **Biodiversity Loss**:  
  Increased PM2.5 and black carbon emissions from data center generators could degrade air quality in Quinte West and Hastings County, affecting plant and animal species reliant on clean air and stable ecosystems.  

## Case Study: Quinte West, Hastings County, and Proposed Data Center Sites  

The proposed data center developments at 7 Riverside Drive (former Saputo Dairy) and 920 Trenton Frankford Road (former Sonoco Paper Mill) in Quinte West, Hastings County, present a unique case for analyzing the localized environmental impacts of hydrocarbon-based generators.  

### Geographical and Ecological Context  

- **Trent River/Bay of Quinte Watershed**:  
  This watershed is a vital ecological corridor, supporting diverse flora and fauna. The Trent River flows into the Bay of Quinte, which is part of Lake Ontario’s ecosystem. The region’s proximity to the Great Lakes makes it particularly sensitive to air and water pollution ^3^.  

- **Quinte West and Hastings County**:  
  These areas are characterized by a mix of urban, agricultural, and natural landscapes. The proposed data center sites are located near residential areas and the Trent River, raising concerns about localized air quality degradation and potential contamination of water resources ^12^.  

### Emissions Scenario Analysis  

If the proposed data centers at 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road are equipped with diesel generators, the following emissions scenarios could occur:  

1. **Localized NOX and PM2.5 Spikes**:  
   - Diesel generators, operating at high load during grid outages, could emit NOX and PM2.5 at levels exceeding local air quality standards. For example, in 2024, Alberta’s transportation sector contributed 16% of its total NOX emissions ^2^. If similar patterns occur in Quinte West, localized NOX concentrations could rise, affecting nearby communities.  

2. **Black Carbon Deposition**:  
   - Black carbon emissions from diesel generators could deposit on the Trent River’s surface, reducing albedo and contributing to warming. This could disrupt aquatic ecosystems and accelerate the melting of ice in nearby wetlands ^2^.  

3. **Health Risks for Residents**:  
   - Residents of Quinte West and Hastings County, including vulnerable populations such as children and the elderly, could face increased health risks from prolonged exposure to PM2.5 and NO2. Studies in Canada have shown that even small increases in PM2.5 concentrations are associated with significant health burdens ^3^.

## Mitigation Strategies and Policy Recommendations  

To minimize the environmental impact of hydrocarbon-based generators in data centers, the following strategies are recommended:  

### 1. **Adoption of Cleaner Fuels**  
   - Transitioning from diesel to cleaner alternatives such as biodiesel or hydrogen could significantly reduce NOX, PM2.5, and black carbon emissions. For example, biodiesel can reduce PM2.5 emissions by up to 20% compared to conventional diesel ^5^.  

### 2. **Implementation of Emission Control Technologies**  
   - Data centers should invest in advanced emission control technologies, such as selective catalytic reduction (SCR) for NOX and diesel particulate filters (DPFs) for PM2.5. These technologies have been shown to reduce emissions in the transportation sector ^8^.  

### 3. **Regulatory Compliance and Monitoring**  
   - Local governments in Quinte West and Hastings County should enforce stringent emissions standards for data centers, requiring real-time monitoring of pollutants. This would ensure compliance with national and provincial air quality standards ^3^.  

### 4. **Community Engagement and Environmental Impact Assessments**  
   - Comprehensive environmental impact assessments (EIAs) should be conducted for the proposed data center sites at 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road. These assessments should include input from local residents, environmental groups, and Indigenous communities to address concerns about air and water quality ^12^.  

## Conclusion  

The environmental impact of hydrocarbon-based generators from data centers is a pressing concern, particularly in ecologically sensitive regions like Quinte West, Hastings County, and the Trent River/Bay of Quinte watershed. While national emissions inventories highlight the dominance of sectors such as oil and gas in pollutant contributions, the localized impact of data center generators cannot be overlooked. Without proactive mitigation measures, the proposed developments in Quinte West could exacerbate air quality degradation, harm aquatic ecosystems, and pose health risks to residents. By adopting cleaner fuels, implementing emission control technologies, and enforcing regulatory standards, stakeholders can ensure that the growth of data infrastructure aligns with environmental sustainability goals.
