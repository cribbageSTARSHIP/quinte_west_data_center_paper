## How a Propylene Glycol Spill Affects the Local Environment (Land and Nearby Rivers)

### Introduction

Propylene glycol (PG), a colorless, odorless, viscous liquid, is widely used in industrial, pharmaceutical, and food applications due to its low toxicity and versatility ^[3]. However, its environmental impact remains a critical concern, particularly in the context of spills affecting land and aquatic ecosystems. This paper examines the ecological consequences of a PG spill, focusing on its effects on soil, vegetation, and nearby rivers, with specific relevance to the Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed, Hastings County, and the proposed sites at 7 Riverside Drive (former Saputo Dairy) and 920 Trenton Frankford Road (former Sonoco Paper Mill). Drawing on authoritative studies, toxicological data, and environmental impact assessments, this analysis evaluates the risks posed by PG spills and their implications for municipal infrastructure and ecological health.

---

### 1. Environmental Toxicity of Propylene Glycol

#### 1.1 Acute and Chronic Toxicity to Aquatic Life

Propylene glycol exhibits lower acute toxicity to aquatic organisms compared to ethylene glycol (EG), but its environmental impact is not negligible. Studies indicate that PG has a **Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)** of **1.68 kg O₂/L**, which can lead to **aquatic hypoxia** at concentrations exceeding **200 mg/L** ^[11]. This hypoxia occurs due to the microbial degradation of PG, which consumes dissolved oxygen in water, potentially suffocating aquatic life. While PG is classified as "practically non-toxic" under EPA criteria for direct poisoning (LC₅₀ > 10,000 mg/L for most fish and invertebrates), its **oxygen-depleting properties** pose a significant threat to aquatic ecosystems, particularly in enclosed or slow-moving water bodies like the Trent River ^[7].

#### 1.2 Soil and Vegetation Impact

On land, PG's **low solubility in soil** (approximately 1.5 g/L) and **high water solubility** (over 500 g/L) create a dual risk: it may remain in the upper soil layers, potentially affecting plant root systems, or leach into groundwater. PG's **moderate adsorption to soil particles** (log Koc ≈ 1.5) suggests limited persistence in soil, but its **biodegradation rate** (half-life of 1–2 weeks in aerobic conditions) depends on environmental factors such as temperature and microbial activity ^[11]. Vegetation exposed to PG may experience **phytotoxic effects**, though studies on plant uptake are limited. However, PG's **low volatility** (vapor pressure < 0.1 mmHg) and **non-flammability** (flash point 103–107°C) reduce immediate fire or air quality risks ^[3].

---

### 2. Aquatic Hypoxia and Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)

#### 2.1 Mechanism of Oxygen Depletion

The degradation of PG in aquatic environments is driven by **aerobic bacteria**, which metabolize PG into **lactic and pyruvic acids**, a process that consumes oxygen. The **BOD of 1.68 kg O₂/L** is significantly higher than that of ethylene glycol (0.8–1.2 kg O₂/L), making PG a more potent oxygen sink in waterways ^[11]. This oxygen depletion can lead to **hypoxia**, a condition where dissolved oxygen levels fall below 2 mg/L, suffocating fish and invertebrates. In the Trent River, which flows through Hastings County and into the Bay of Quinte, such hypoxia could disrupt **aquatic food webs** and **biodiversity**, particularly in areas with low water flow or high organic matter content.

#### 2.2 Inhibitor Additives and Mitigation

Inhibitor additives, such as **corrosion inhibitors or biocides**, can reduce PG's BOD by **15–20%** by slowing microbial degradation ^[11]. However, these additives may introduce secondary pollutants, complicating environmental risk assessments. For the proposed sites at 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road, proximity to the Trent River necessitates **spill containment protocols** that account for both PG and its additives, especially in scenarios involving industrial storage or transportation.

---

### 3. Toxicological and Health Effects

#### 3.1 Human and Animal Exposure

While PG is **GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe)** by the FDA for food and pharmaceutical use, **acute exposure** to high concentrations (e.g., 23,500 mg/kg in rats) can cause **gastrointestinal effects** such as **hemorrhagic enteritis** ^[5]. Chronic exposure studies in rats (2,500 mg/kg/day for 23 months) showed **no cardiovascular or hematological effects**, but **high-dose oral exposure** (7,904 mg/kg in horses) led to **myocardial edema** ^[5]. For humans, **dermal exposure** to undiluted PG can cause **irritation** and **allergic reactions** in 12.5% of test subjects, with seasonal variation observed in winter months ^[9]. These findings highlight the need for **occupational safety measures** at industrial sites like the former Saputo Dairy and Sonoco Paper Mill.

#### 3.2 Ecological Risks

PG's **low acute toxicity** to aquatic organisms does not eliminate its risks. **Long-term exposure** to PG in water can disrupt **reproductive cycles** and **embryonic development** in fish, though data on this are limited ^[10]. In soil, PG's **moderate persistence** and **potential for leaching** could contaminate groundwater, affecting **drinking water sources** in Hastings County. The Trent River, a critical habitat for species like the **Atlantic salmon** and **lake sturgeon**, would face heightened risks from spills near its tributaries.

### 4. Case Study: Environmental Impact on the Trent River / Bay of Quinte Watershed

#### 4.1 Geographical and Hydrological Context

The **Trent River** flows through **Hastings County**, forming part of the **Bay of Quinte** estuary, a vital ecosystem supporting **wetlands, fisheries, and migratory bird habitats**. The proposed sites at **7 Riverside Drive** (adjacent to the Trent River) and **920 Trenton Frankford Road** (near the Bay of Quinte) are located in areas with **high ecological sensitivity**. A PG spill at either site could rapidly contaminate the river, given the **low elevation** and **high water table** in these regions.

#### 4.2 Scenario Analysis: Spill at 7 Riverside Drive

A **1,000-liter PG spill** at 7 Riverside Drive would release approximately **1,000 kg of PG** into the surrounding environment. Assuming **50% of the spill reaches the Trent River**, this would result in **500 kg of PG entering the river**, potentially causing **hypoxia** in a **10-km stretch** of the river. The **BOD of 1.68 kg O₂/L** would deplete oxygen levels to critical thresholds within **48 hours**, leading to **fish kills** and **algal blooms**. Soil contamination at the site could persist for **1–2 weeks**, affecting **vegetation** and **groundwater quality**.

#### 4.3 Scenario Analysis: Spill at 920 Trenton Frankford Road

A **500-liter PG spill** at 920 Trenton Frankford Road, located near the **Bay of Quinte**, would pose risks to **estuarine ecosystems**. PG's **high solubility** and **low adsorption** to sediments would allow it to **disperse rapidly** into the Bay, affecting **brackish water species** and **shellfish populations**. The **BOD impact** would be amplified by the **high organic content** of estuarine sediments, potentially leading to **long-term hypoxia** in the Bay of Quinte.

### 5. Mitigation Strategies and Regulatory Considerations

#### 5.1 Spill Prevention and Response

To mitigate PG spill risks, **double containment systems** and **real-time monitoring** are essential at industrial sites. The **City of Quinte West** and **Hastings County** should enforce **spill response plans** that include **absorbent booms**, **activated carbon filtration**, and **aeration systems** to counteract hypoxia in affected waterways ^[11]. For the Trent River, **buffer zones** of at least **50 meters** should be established around industrial sites to reduce direct contamination.

#### 5.2 Regulatory Framework

Current regulations under the **Canadian Environmental Protection Act (CEPA)** and **Ontario’s Environmental Protection Act (EPA)** classify PG as a **non-toxic substance** but require **spill reporting** and **environmental impact assessments** for large quantities. However, the **BOD risk** of PG is not explicitly addressed in these frameworks, necessitating **updated guidelines** for industrial facilities near sensitive ecosystems like the Trent River.

### 7. Conclusion and Recommendations

A propylene glycol spill poses significant risks to the **Trent River / Bay of Quinte watershed**, primarily through **aquatic hypoxia** and **soil contamination**. While PG is less toxic than ethylene glycol, its **high BOD** and **oxygen-depleting properties** necessitate stringent spill prevention measures. For the **City of Quinte West** and **Hastings County**, the following actions are recommended:

1. **Enforce strict spill containment protocols** at industrial sites like 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road.
2. **Update environmental regulations** to address PG’s BOD risks in aquatic ecosystems.
3. **Conduct regular ecological monitoring** of the Trent River and Bay of Quinte for early detection of contamination.
4. **Promote bio-based PG alternatives** to further reduce environmental impact ^[1].

By integrating these strategies, municipal and industrial stakeholders can safeguard the ecological integrity of the Trent River watershed while balancing industrial needs.
