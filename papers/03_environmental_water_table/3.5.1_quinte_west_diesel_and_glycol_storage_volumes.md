## Projected On-Site Storage Volumes of Diesel and Propylene Glycol in Quinte West: A Multi-Disciplinary Analysis

The area has been identified as a potential site for infrastructure projects, including energy and chemical storage facilities, due to its proximity to transportation networks and existing industrial zones. Two key locations under scrutiny are **7 Riverside Drive** (former Saputo Dairy) and **920 Trenton Frankford Road** (former Sonoco Paper Mill), both of which have historical ties to chemical use and storage. This paper evaluates the projected on-site storage volumes of **diesel** and **propylene glycol** at these sites, synthesizing toxicological, environmental, and municipal infrastructure data to inform risk assessments and regulatory planning.

### Section 1: Diesel Storage Volumes in Quinte West  

#### 1.1. Contextualizing Diesel Storage in Municipal Infrastructure  
Diesel fuel is a critical component of Quinte West’s energy and transportation systems, with applications ranging from emergency generators to industrial machinery. Storage volumes are typically determined by **regulatory standards**, **facility size**, and **risk mitigation protocols**. #### 1.2. Environmental and Health Risks of Diesel Storage  
Diesel storage poses risks through **leakage**, **runoff**, and **airborne emissions**, particularly in proximity to water bodies. The Trent River, a key waterway in Quinte West, is vulnerable to contamination from improper storage practices.  

- **Soil and Water Contamination**: Diesel leaks can lead to **soil saturation** with polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), which are persistent pollutants. Runoff into the Trent River could threaten aquatic life and drinking water sources ^[11]^.  
- **Health Impacts**: Inhalation of diesel particulate matter is linked to **respiratory diseases** and **cardiovascular effects**, though these are more relevant to occupational exposure than on-site storage per se ^[11]^.  

#### 1.3. Mitigation Strategies for Diesel Storage  
To minimize risks, Quinte West must adopt **best practices** such as:  
- **Secondary containment systems** to capture leaks.  
- **Regular inspections** of storage tanks and pipelines.  
- **Buffer zones** between storage areas and the Trent River.

### Section 2: Propylene Glycol Storage Volumes and Environmental Implications  

#### 2.1. Overview of Propylene Glycol Use and Storage  
Propylene glycol (PG) is a **common industrial chemical** used in antifreeze, food additives, and pharmaceuticals. Its **low toxicity** compared to ethylene glycol makes it a preferred substitute, though its environmental behavior remains a concern. In Quinte West, PG storage may be required for **industrial processes** or **emergency preparedness** at sites like 7 Riverside Drive and 920 Trenton Frankford Road.  

#### 2.2. Projected Storage Volumes  
While the provided corpus does not specify PG storage volumes for Quinte West, industry benchmarks and toxicological data can inform estimates:  
- **Industrial Facilities**: Facilities requiring PG for cooling or chemical processes may store **5,000–20,000 liters** on-site.  
- **Emergency Reserves**: For fire suppression or backup systems, storage could range up to **10,000 liters**.  

#### 2.3. Environmental Degradation and Persistence of Propylene Glycol  
The biodegradation rates of PG in soils and water are critical to assessing its environmental impact.  

- **Soil Biodegradation**:  
  - At concentrations **<6,000 mg/kg**, PG degrades at **2.3–93.3 mg/kg/day**, depending on temperature and soil type (e.g., 2.3 mg/kg/day at -2°C, 93.3 mg/kg/day at 25°C) ^[5]^.  
  - This suggests that PG stored at the former Sonoco Paper Mill (920 Trenton Frankford Road) could be **rapidly degraded** in soils, though **high concentrations** (e.g., >6,000 mg/kg) may persist longer.  

- **Aquatic Systems**: PG is **readily biodegradable** in surface waters, with **no significant abiotic transformation** observed ^[5]^. However, runoff into the Trent River could temporarily elevate PG levels, though dilution and microbial activity would mitigate long-term risks.  

#### 2.4. Health and Safety Considerations  
PG is generally **non-toxic** in low doses but can cause **dermal irritation** and **allergic reactions** at high concentrations ^[1]^.  

- **Dermal Exposure**: Studies show that **12.5% of patients** exposed to undiluted PG exhibited **irritant or allergic reactions**, with **70%** of cases classified as **primary irritation** ^[1]^. This underscores the need for **safety protocols** at storage sites.  
- **Ingestion and Inhalation**: While PG is **safe in food and cosmetics**, acute ingestion of **>2,500 mg/kg** can lead to **metabolic acidosis** and **osmolal gap** in humans, though such exposure is unlikely in controlled storage environments ^[4]^.
